# Onboard, Don't Program

**Category:** Conversational  
**Status:** Published

---

## Name
Onboard, Don't Program

## Context
Extended AI-assisted work sessions where the model needs to understand your context, role, constraints, and preferences before producing useful output. Applies at the start of any project, engagement, or recurring workflow where the AI will be a consistent collaborator.

## Problem
Users treat AI like a search engine — one prompt, one answer, repeat. Each interaction starts cold. The model has no context about who you are, what you're building, what constraints you operate under, or how you make decisions. Result: generic output, repeated corrections, and constant re-explanation of things that shouldn't need re-explaining.

## Solution
Open extended AI sessions the way you'd onboard a new team member. Provide a structured context document at the start:
- **Who you are**: Role, industry, experience level
- **What you're working on**: Project, goal, current state
- **Constraints**: Budget, timeline, technology stack, regulatory environment
- **Preferences**: Communication style, level of detail, terminology
- **Decision criteria**: What does "good" look like in your context

This isn't programming or jailbreaking. It's professional context-setting. The model produces work that fits your actual situation instead of a hypothetical average one.

## Value Consequence
Outputs arrive already calibrated to your context. The quality gap between "what AI produces" and "what I actually need" shrinks dramatically. Less time correcting; more time building. The investment in onboarding pays forward across every subsequent interaction in that session.

---

## Notes
This pattern scales into persistent system prompts for teams and organizations. A shared onboarding document becomes a team's shared AI context — reducing variance in outputs and ensuring every team member's AI sessions start from the same foundation.
