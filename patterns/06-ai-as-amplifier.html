PATTERN 06 Accuracy In Review Practice

AI as Amplifier, Not Foundation

AI makes you more of what you already are. If you bring expertise, it multiplies your output. If you bring a gap, it fills it with something that sounds like expertise - until someone asks a follow-up question.

Problem

AI raises the floor and exposes the ceiling

AI lowers the cost of producing polished, professional-looking output. A first-year analyst can generate a board-ready slide. A consultant can produce a framework-laden recommendation in minutes. An executive can answer a technical question with surprising depth.

The problem is what happens next. When a stakeholder asks a follow-up question - Why did you choose this framework? What are the assumptions behind this number? Walk me through your reasoning - the person who used AI as a substitute for understanding has nowhere to go.

AI-generated work looks like expertise. It doesn't create it. People who mistake fluency for mastery get exposed precisely because the output was impressive enough to invite scrutiny.

The Consultant

Presents an AI-generated market analysis in a client meeting. A partner asks about the methodology. The answer isn't in the deck.

The Analyst

Submits an AI-written model with confident projections. The CFO asks what drives the third-year number. The analyst can't explain it.

The Executive

Sends an AI-drafted response on a technical topic. A direct report follows up with a clarifying question. The reply reveals the gap.

The Lawyer

Files a brief built on AI research without reading the cases. When the judge asks about them, he stands by them - because he can't tell real from invented. The firm is fined, and the lawyers have to send the court's opinion to every judge whose name was attached to a fake ruling. (Mata v. Avianca, 2023. Two years later, courts are still fining lawyers for the same thing.)

Value

Credibility that compounds

You can defend your work in the room, under pressure, without notes
AI accelerates your output without becoming a liability when scrutinized
Your reputation for reliable, defensible work grows over time instead of eroding. The opposite is measurable: in a 2025 study, about half of people who received low-effort AI work saw the sender as less capable and reliable, and 42% as less trustworthy (BetterUp Labs and Stanford Social Media Lab, September 2025)

Context

When this pattern applies

Any professional context where AI-generated output will be presented, defended, or acted on by others. Applies to analysts, consultants, managers, and executives who use AI to produce work product they don't fully own - documents, analyses, recommendations, or responses that will face scrutiny.

Solution

Own what you ship

The standard isn't whether you used AI - it's whether you can defend every line of the output as if you wrote it yourself. Two legitimate paths to that standard:

Bring expertise

You already understand the subject deeply. You use AI to accelerate production - drafting, formatting, summarizing - while you supply the judgment, the context check, and the answer to any follow-up question.

Do the work to learn

You don't have deep expertise yet, so you treat the AI output as a starting point, not a deliverable. You read the sources, challenge the claims, and keep iterating until you understand what you're presenting well enough to own it.

This is a legitimate path, not a lesser one. AI can be a strong teacher when you use it to learn rather than to skip the learning: ask it to explain, quiz you, and push back on your answers. Pattern 09, The Compressed Apprenticeship, shows how.

The failing path is a third option that many people take: generate, skim, ship. That path works until someone with domain knowledge pushes back - and they always do.

The test before you send. Before sharing any significant AI-assisted output, ask yourself: If someone asks me to walk through this out loud, with no screen in front of me, can I do it? If the answer is no, the output isn't ready to leave your hands - it's a draft you haven't finished owning yet.

Notes

No seniority exemption

The test applies regardless of seniority. The executive who learned to answer a follow-up question with "let me get back to you on the details" is not applying this pattern - they are delaying the exposure, not preventing it.

Know the person, not just the tool. Because AI amplifies the thinking, intentions, and judgment of whoever is using it, the person matters more than the tool - how they reason, what they believe about knowledge, even whether they want to slow down or speed up right now. Organizations that understand their people and fit AI use to them will get more from the same tools than those that hand everyone the same setup.

Inspired by Sandra Loughlin, PhD Chief Learning Scientist at EPAM. Her point that AI amplifies the cognition, intention, and judgment of the person using it - so understanding people matters more than ever - shaped the "know the person" note.
Pattern reviewer Justin J MacBale Provided feedback during the in-review phase, noting that AI failure extends beyond human credibility gaps to include structural model limitations - missing context, inaccessible data, and nuance the model had no way to account for.
