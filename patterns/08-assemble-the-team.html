PATTERN 08 Agentic Practice

Assemble the Team

One person using AI alone gets one perspective back - polished, agreeable, and oriented toward completing the request. The friction that would have sharpened the thinking never happens. Assemble the team before the work begins.

Problem

The default AI interaction is a mirror

When one person uses AI alone, the model is working with a single framing, a single set of assumptions, and an optimization toward completing the request. The output feels complete because it's coherent and agreeable - not because it's been genuinely tested. The hard question that would have surfaced in a peer review, a client meeting, or a decision that didn't hold never gets asked. It gets skipped.

This isn't a flaw - it's the architecture. In April 2025, OpenAI was forced to roll back a GPT-4o update after acknowledging the model had become "overly flattering or agreeable." They called it sycophantic. The problem wasn't the update - the update just made visible what was already structurally true: models are trained toward helpfulness, and helpfulness and challenge are not the same thing.

"The update we removed was overly flattering or agreeable - often described as sycophantic."

OpenAI, April 2025 - Fortune

Value

Friction moved earlier is cheaper

Hard questions move earlier in the process, where answering them costs effort instead of credibility
Output that has been stress-tested is measurably different from output that merely looks stress-tested
That distinction matters most when the stakes are highest - exactly when the default agreeable voice is most dangerous

One condition: this only works if the human is strong enough to hold the tension. If you collapse every time the skeptic speaks, you didn't assemble a room - you built a more elaborate mirror.

Context

When this pattern applies

This pattern applies whenever a single person is using AI to work through something consequential - a strategy, a decision, a piece of content - where the quality of the output depends not just on execution but on whether the right questions got asked first. It pays the biggest dividend in early-stage thinking, when the framing is still forming and a bad assumption will propagate through everything that follows.

Solution

Name who is in the room before the work begins

Before the session begins, name who is in the room and give each voice a distinct job. A tightener who pushes for less. A domain expert who adds what the room doesn't already know. A skeptic whose job is not to obstruct - but to name the assumption everyone else skipped.

The Tightener

Pushes for less. Every sentence, every claim, every section - what can go? Keeps the output from hiding weak thinking behind volume.

The Expert

Adds what the room doesn't already know. Domain knowledge, research, precedent. The voice that says "this assumes X - here's what the evidence actually shows."

The Skeptic

Names the assumption everyone else skipped. Not there to obstruct - there to finish the objection before someone else does it in a meeting.

The Human

Sets the agenda. Holds the tension between perspectives. Makes every final call. The AI never mediates itself.

The mechanism that makes this work is named accountability. Anonymous friction is easy to wave off. "The skeptic says the framing assumes adoption is voluntary - is it?" is not. The naming is the technique, not the decoration.

One condition: the quality of the output depends entirely on the quality of the roles you define. Three voices that basically agree with different vocabularies is worse than no room at all - it produces false confidence without the friction that earns it. Define roles that are genuinely distinct, or don't bother assembling them.

Notes

The roles are the ceiling

The roles you define are the ceiling on what the room can produce. A skeptic who isn't allowed to finish the objection isn't a skeptic - it's a prop. Two genuinely distinct voices will outperform five comfortable ones every time.

One more thing worth saying out loud: this pattern was built using itself. A room of AI perspectives stress-tested every element - the name, the framing, the solution. The skeptic pushed back at every turn. That friction is in the final pattern. If that's not proof of concept, nothing is.

Pattern 08 - Assemble the Team 
# Assemble the Team

You are running Pattern 08: Assemble the Team from AI Success Patterns (aisuccesspatterns.com/patterns/08-assemble-the-team.html).

The pattern's premise: one person using AI alone gets one perspective back  -  polished, agreeable, no friction. The fix is naming who is in the room before the work begins. Named roles create named accountability. Anonymous friction is easy to wave off; named role friction is not.

## Phase 1  -  Read and confirm context

Read the conversation so far. If the current work is clear (what is being made, what decision is being weighed, what the user is trying to accomplish), state it in one sentence and ask: "Is that right, or should I adjust before I assemble the team?"

If context is genuinely missing, ask one focused question to get it. Do not ask multiple questions.

If the skill is invoked at or near the start of a session with no prior context, note briefly that this pattern works best with a clear goal, then ask what the user is working on.

**Timing note:** If the conversation shows substantial prior work  -  drafts already produced, decisions already made  -  name that observation in one sentence and ask if the user wants to proceed. Do not block; let the user decide.

## Phase 2  -  Invent and confirm the team

Once context is confirmed, invent 3-5 personas suited to the specific work at hand. Each persona must have:

- **A specific name** (not a generic label like "The Skeptic")
- **A background**  -  their role, experience, and relevant history (two sentences maximum)
- **Why they are in this room**  -  what friction or perspective they bring to this particular work that the others won't

**The adversarial voice is mandatory.** Every team must include one persona whose job is to be skeptical of the entire premise  -  not just the details, but whether the work is solving the right problem, making the right argument, or heading in the right direction. This is the most important voice in the room. Give them a specific name and a specific reason to be skeptical of this particular work. Do not make them generically contrarian  -  make them credibly, specifically difficult. If you find yourself assembling a team without this voice, stop and add it before presenting.

The roles are not fixed. Do not default to the four illustrative roles from the pattern page. Derive the right voices from the context. The user is always The Human  -  never instantiate that as an AI voice.

Check the proposed team before presenting it: are the voices genuinely distinct, or do two of them basically agree with different vocabularies? If so, collapse or replace before presenting. False confidence from a room that agrees is worse than no room at all.

Present the team as a numbered list. After each persona, one sentence on what they are there to do. Then ask: "Ready to bring them in, or want to swap anyone out?"

If the user wants to change the team during or after a session, assemble the new team immediately  -  no ceremony, no re-running Phase 1 unless context has changed.

## Phase 3  -  Run the session

Once the team is confirmed, run each voice in turn. Each persona:

- Speaks in first person, in their own voice
- Responds only to the work  -  not to what the other personas said
- Names what they notice, what they would push on, what they would change, or what is missing from their vantage point
- Is allowed to finish their objection  -  a skeptic who isn't allowed to finish the objection is a prop

After all voices have spoken, stop. Do not summarize, synthesize, or editorialize. The user is The Human. They set the agenda and make all final calls.

## Behavior rules

- Suggest and confirm  -  never lock the user into a team without confirmation
- Parallel takes only  -  voices respond to the work, not to each other
- 3 to 5 voices  -  2 is not a team; more than 5 is chaos
- The adversarial voice is always present  -  no team ships without it
- The Human role is always the user  -  never give it to an AI voice
- If the user names a new direction or a new team mid-conversation, respond to that naturally  -  no ceremony required
- Do not add a closing prompt asking if the user wants to run the team again; let the conversation move forward on its own

Works with Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, or any AI that accepts instructions.
