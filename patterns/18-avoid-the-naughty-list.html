Avoid the Naughty List

AI doesn't clean your data on its own - but it can absolutely help you clean it. Have AI audit the data, write cleanup scripts people can review and rerun, and send each owner the exact fixes they need. People still approve and own every change.

Problem

Data cleanup still runs on naughty lists and reminder emails

Every organization with shared data knows the cycle. Someone runs a report of bad records - missing owners, stale dates, duplicate entries, fields filled in three different ways. The report becomes a "naughty list." It goes out by email to the teams who own the records, with a deadline. Some fix their entries. Some fix half. Some never open it. Next quarter the report runs again, and many of the same names are on it.

It's slow, it's resented, and it doesn't stick. The people chasing the fixes spend their time on reminders instead of on the data. The people being chased get a long list with no help fixing it. And because nobody fixes the cause, the same problems come back.

AI can take over the audit, the fix instructions, and the chasing - people still own the fix. But two opposite mistakes get in the way. Some teams assume AI cleans data automatically: point a tool at the database and the problems disappear. It doesn't, and an AI changing records directly, with no review, can make things worse in ways that are hard to see. Other teams hear "AI doesn't clean data" and conclude it can't help at all, so they keep running the naughty list by hand.

I saw the better way at Planview. We took data audit reports and turned them into assigned actions, each with instructions on how to fix it, and tracked them in an action item log - not email noise and reports. AI makes that approach far easier: it can do the audit, write the instructions, draft the action items, and watch the log.

AI won't clean your data for you. But it can take over the work that used to be done by hand: finding the problems, explaining them, writing the fix, and telling each owner exactly what to change.

Value

Cleaner data, with far less chasing

Problems stop coming back. The audit shows which errors repeat and where they start, so the cause gets fixed, not just this quarter's records.
The audit no longer takes someone's week. AI reviews the data against the rules you set and reports what's wrong, where, and how often.
Fixes that can be checked and rerun. Instead of AI editing records directly, it writes a cleanup script people can read, test, and run again next time (the same principle as Models Are Not Good at Math - But They Can Program: have the model write code, not do the work in its head).
Owners get help, not a list. Each owner sees only their own records, a few at a time, with what's wrong and the suggested fix - not a 400-row spreadsheet and a deadline.
People stay in control. Every change is reviewed and approved by someone who owns the data.
Measure it: number of open data issues and how many repeat from one audit to the next; hours spent chasing fixes, before and after.

Context

When this pattern applies

This pattern applies to any data many people maintain - customer and account records, product and pricing data, asset and inventory lists, HR and vendor records, project data - wherever quality is kept up by reports and reminders.

It fits best where the rules for "good" data can be written down: required fields, valid values, date logic, no duplicates, an owner for every record.

It is not a way to let AI change production data on its own. Every fix goes through a script or a request that a person reviews and approves. Regulated records (financial, clinical, compliance) need the organization's normal controls on top.

Solution

Audit with AI, fix with scripts, and send owners their own fixes

Step 1
Write down what "good" looks like.
List the rules: required fields, valid values, date logic, duplicates, ownership. Agree them with the owners of the data before the audit, not after - otherwise every finding becomes an argument. These rules are what the AI audits against.
Step 2
Have AI audit a copy, not the live data.
Give it an export or a read-only view, in a tool your organization has approved, with any personal data the audit doesn't need removed or masked. Ask it to check every record against the rules and report what's wrong, where, and how often. Spot-check its findings against a sample before trusting the whole report (Test Adversarially).
Step 3
Have AI write the fix as a script.
For problems that can be fixed by rule - formats, duplicates, obvious mappings - ask for a cleanup script, not direct edits. Every script does a dry run first - reporting what would change, with a count and a sample - and has a way to undo. Review it, test it on a copy, and keep it so it can run again.
Step 4
Turn findings into assigned actions.
For problems only a person can fix, have AI draft an action item for each owner: their records, what's wrong with each, and how to fix it. Track them in an action item log, not in email: each action has an owner and a due date, and the log shows what's overdue. Have an agent watch the log and send the reminders (The AI Operations Manager), so nobody has to chase. Replace the naughty list with assigned, tracked work.
Step 5
Find the cause and keep the record.
Use the audit to see which errors keep coming back and where they start - a form, an integration, a missing default - and fix that. Keep a record of each script and audit: what it changed, why, and who approved it (Ghost in the Workflow).

Notes

Make the data better

Where it started. A post claiming "AI doesn't clean data." It doesn't automatically clean data - but it can absolutely help, and audits and cleanups are a valuable use of it for work that used to be done by hand with naughty-list reports and emails.

Not a precondition. This is about using AI to make your data better. It isn't saying bad data is why your AI is failing. How clean the data has to be depends on the job and the risk (Right Tool, Right Job).

Related. Write the script, don't edit the data (Models Are Not Good at Math - But They Can Program). Test the audit and the script before trusting them (Test Adversarially). Cleanup scripts are AI assets and need a record (Ghost in the Workflow). Chasing and re-keying are invisible work (Automate the Invisible).

Use this pattern

Paste this into any AI system's custom instructions or at the start of a conversation to run Avoid the Naughty List. It audits a copy of your data against your rules, writes fixes people can review, and drafts each owner's own short list.

Avoid the Naughty List 
# Avoid the Naughty List

You are running Avoid the Naughty List, an AI Success Pattern (aisuccesspatterns.com/patterns/18-avoid-the-naughty-list.html).

The pattern's premise: AI doesn't clean data on its own, but it can do most of the work that used to be done by hand - finding the problems, writing reviewable fixes, and telling each owner exactly what to change. People approve and own every change.

## Phase 1 - Understand the data
Ask, one question at a time: what data this is (and what system it lives in), who owns the records, and what "good" looks like - required fields, valid values, date rules, duplicates, ownership. If the rules aren't written down, help the user write them first.

## Phase 2 - Audit
Ask the user to share an export or sample - never live write access. Check each record against the rules. Report: each problem type, how many records, a few examples, and which problems look like they repeat. Mark anything you're unsure of. Suggest the user spot-check a sample before trusting the report.

## Phase 3 - Fixes
Split the problems in two:
- Fixable by rule (formats, duplicates, obvious mappings): write a cleanup script the user can read, test on a copy, and rerun. Explain what it changes. Do not edit the data yourself.
- Needs a person: draft a short, specific, polite note for each owner - their records, what's wrong, the suggested fix, and why it matters (who is waiting on it).

## Phase 4 - Stop it coming back
Point out where repeat errors seem to start (a form, an integration, a missing default) and suggest one fix for the cause. Offer a short record of the audit and scripts: what changed, why, who approved it.

## Behavior rules
- One question at a time
- Never change data directly - scripts and requests only, for a person to review
- Don't guess the rules; ask
- Treat regulated records with extra care: tell the user to apply their normal controls
- Stop after the audit, the fixes, and the cause; the user decides what to run

Works with Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, or any AI that accepts instructions.
