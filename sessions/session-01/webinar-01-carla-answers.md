The number that has to hold up on slide 12: **20 percent** (quote-turnaround time). Answers 3 and 4 are where it comes from.

1. **Why are we doing AI at all?**
Our quoting team is buried. A custom quote takes four days, and we lose deals to competitors who answer in two. AI isn't the strategy. Faster quotes are.

2. **What did we try first?**
Last spring a few engineers started using a free chatbot on their own. Nobody approved it and nobody knew what went into it. That's how I found out we had an AI program - from IT, not from a plan.

3. **What actually worked?**
We ran a 60-day pilot with three estimators. AI drafted the first pass of the quote from past jobs, and the estimator checked it. Turnaround dropped from four days to just over three.

4. **Where does the 20 percent come from?**
Four days down to 3.2, across 140 quotes in the pilot. That's the pilot number, not a promise for the whole company. I'd expect less at first when we roll it out wider.

5. **What failed?**
The AI priced two jobs with steel costs from last year. The estimators caught both. That's why a person signs every quote - that rule doesn't change.

6. **What's the risk the board should worry about?**
Customer drawings going into tools we don't control. We've stopped that. Approved tools only, and customer files stay inside our systems.

7. **What does it cost?**
About $60,000 in year one: licenses, two months of an outside consultant, and training. One won deal covers it.

8. **What are you asking the board for?**
Approval to expand the pilot to all twelve estimators in the first quarter, and a check-in at the June meeting with the real numbers.

9. **One opinion you'd defend in this room?**
AI won't replace our estimators. The estimators who use it will replace the quotes that take four days.

10. **What would make you stop?**
If quote accuracy drops - if we see more than two pricing errors a month that reach a customer - we pause and fix it before we go further.
