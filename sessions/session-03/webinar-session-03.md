**Act III - The Hero Moment (2:30-7:30) - live, on screen**
- "Let's rewind to Monday. Here's what Carla should have done." Slide 3: "Name who's in the room." Switch to the screen.
- "The prompt is on the page - the link is below."
- **Say it plainly first:** "AI isn't trying to fool you. It's trying to help. Helping and challenging aren't the same thing."
- **And say what this is:** "This is a rehearsal. It finds questions. It doesn't prove you're right." [1]
- "Isn't this a red team? Close. A red team is one way to run it." [11]
- Paste the Assemble the Team "Use this pattern" text into a fresh AI session, then Carla's proposal (see paste-ins).
- **Phase 1 - confirm the context.** The AI says what it thinks the work is in one sentence. Carla says yes.
- **Phase 2 - the team.** The AI invents three to five named people. Read the names aloud. Stop on the adversarial voice. "This one's mandatory. Their job is to argue the whole thing is wrong."
  - Point out the human isn't on the list: "Carla's not a persona. She's the one who decides."
  - It's a live run, so the names will differ. Use what comes back.
- **Phase 3 - the room.** Each voice speaks. Slow down on the skeptic and let the objection finish. The hope is it gets near the custom-work question.
  - **Recording rule [3]:** if the take never reaches it, add the voice on screen: "Who should be here that isn't? Add the person who runs the second plant." Then Act V uses the "Carla thought to add him" line below, not the AI-found-it line.
- **Phase 4 - Carla's calls.** Take one objection she accepts, one she rejects, and say why. "The output isn't the comments. It's her decisions." Write them in the project record - that's The Chat Is Not Your Memory. [7]
- Line to land: "Five minutes ago she had a yes-man. Now she has a room, and she still makes every call."

**Act IV - The Value Hypothesis (7:30-9:00)**
- Slide 4. A test, not a promise: run it once, on the next thing going to someone who can say no.
- **The one measure on screen:** "How much came back after the real review?" Count the rework.
- **The evidence:** this isn't flattery by accident. In April 2025 OpenAI rolled back a ChatGPT update it described as overly flattering or agreeable. And when MIT and Google DeepMind researchers had AI models debate each other's answers, accuracy and reasoning improved over a single model.
- Be straight about it: that research was models debating, not a cast of characters in one chat. Same idea, not the same test. That's why you measure your own rework.
- **What it would have cost (Greg, Nov 5; [4]):** "Also count what it would have cost to actually assemble this team. And count the fewer pre-meetings. Some of them are how you get the votes. But we have all lived through death by pre-meeting." [GREG: your death-by-pre-meeting line.]
  - **Spoken only, not on the slide [5]:** "Four project managers in a room is about $200 an hour, in pay alone." Source is on the session page: four project management specialists at the US median of $102,320 a year (Bureau of Labor Statistics) is about $49 an hour each. ## Session page copy (draft)
**A Ten-Minute Taco 'bout AI - Session 3: Assemble the Team**
Carla asks AI what it thinks of her proposal, and AI thinks it's great. Then a board member asks the question it never did. In this session, Greg runs the fix live: name who's in the room, include one voice whose job is to argue against you, and keep every decision for yourself.
**Measure it yourself:** how much rework comes back after the real review, before and after, and how many pre-meetings you skipped. For scale: four project management specialists at the US median of $102,320 a year (Bureau of Labor Statistics) comes to about $200 an hour in the room, pay only, not benefits ($102,320 / 2,080 hours x 4). OpenAI rolled back an overly agreeable ChatGPT update in April 2025. Researchers at MIT and Google DeepMind found models that debate each other's answers improve on a single model, though that study tested debating models, not personas in one chat.
