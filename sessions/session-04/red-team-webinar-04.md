## Round 1 - each voice on the script and slides

**Joan Mercer (adversarial).** I'll say it plainly: the session's premise is that a better process makes AI-drafted comments honest. It doesn't. "I post every comment myself" is about who clicks the button, not who wrote the words. The softened reveal - "a routine I run" - makes it worse, because it now sounds like a hedge. If Greg is going to stand behind this, the session has to say out loud what share of the comment is his: the slide 4 swap shows one line replaced, which tells viewers one human sentence is enough. Second, the Act II villain is the extension that posts. That's the easy target. The harder truth is that Carla's AI drafts in Act III would still get "Did a bot write this?" from me if she only swaps one line. Third, the earnings call example is better than the layoff post - less cruel - but the comment "Exciting times ahead!" under a missed quarter still reads like a gag; I'd rather see the bot comment that's almost right, because that's the one people actually post. Finally: Own the Thinking was the one pattern that answered my objection, and it's been dropped. Without it, the string has no step where the person's own thinking is the point.

**Tom Brandt.** The one comment I see on slide 4 - "we had him quote ten real jobs out loud while we recorded" - is the kind I'd answer. It's specific and it's from someone who's done it. Everything else in the session is about Carla's time, not about me. Act V's purchasing manager reply is believable, but two weeks to a meeting is optimistic; say "a reply" is the win and the meeting is a bonus. On the earnings call post: a CFO is not who a parts maker comments to. If the point is the bot failure, fine, but put the post where Carla's buyers are - an operations VP or plant manager after a tough quarter. And on slide 6, "Conversations started" is the right measure; "profile visits" isn't - nobody I'd buy from counts those.

**Lena Fox.** Timing first. Part A (Onboard) 1:15 and Part B (record, fresh chat) 1:00 are two live AI calls plus a new chat in 2:15 - that's tight; the fresh-chat answer in Part B is the most likely retake. Part D is labeled "live or slide 5" - pick one before recording, or the edit has two versions to choose from. Slides: slide 3's six beads in salsa red on the statement slide are readable, but the dashed beads in blush on red are low contrast at phone size; the "solid = live, dashed = talk track" key is only in the notes, so viewers won't know what dashed means. Slide 4 has three cards plus the before-and-after - the most text in the deck; at phone size the 28px card text will be hard. The struck line on slide 4 is invented ("documentation is key") but the script never says the AI wrote it - say so, or a viewer thinks Carla wrote it. The title slide says "Seven Patterns" and slide 3 shows six - a viewer who counts will notice in the first three minutes. The table on slide 5 is fine, but it duplicates what Greg fills in live; if Part D is live, slide 5 should not appear at all.

**Kim Duong.** I followed Acts I and II easily. In Act III I lost track at "the first two and the last one" - I didn't know there was a list until I saw the beads. Six names in ten minutes: I'll remember "record" and "new hire," because those are words I use. I won't remember "Ghost in the Workflow" from one sentence. "The Chat Is Not Your Memory" landed best because I saw it - new chat, same helper. The one ask at the end ("write down which patterns it already uses") assumes I know the patterns; I'd do it if the page had a one-line list I could print. "You have a stray" is the line I'll repeat at work. I didn't understand "Past 24 hours filter" on slide 4 - I don't use LinkedIn search. And I don't know what "fit check" means.

## Round 2 - each voice on what's still open after a second pass

**Joan Mercer (adversarial).** Second pass, the thing I'd still push on: the session never says when NOT to use this. A comment on a grief post, a layoff, a health post - the routine should skip those, and the AI's fit check is where that rule belongs. If it isn't said, someone will run this under the next layoff post and it'll be the Act II story again with a better draft. I'd also drop "nothing ever posts for you" from the page copy as a selling point - it's the floor, not the feature.

**Tom Brandt.** Second pass: the briefing in paste-in 1 is good - "one specific thing from the post and one from my experience" is exactly what works on me. Put that line on a slide; it's the most useful sentence in the session and it's only in a paste-in viewers will scroll past.

**Lena Fox.** Second pass: the recording rule for Part B (if the fresh chat misses the link rule) says "point at the line, and move on" - that's a good save, but write the exact words Greg says. Act V's slide has the taco callback only in the notes; if the title count changes, the line "Seven ingredients" has to change in three places (title slide, notes, Taco Episode 4) - keep a list. Also the deck has no slide for the session page link ("the prompts are on the page") - a URL on the last slide would help viewers who watch without the page.

**Kim Duong.** Second pass: the measure slide says "[ ___ ]" in big type - that read to me like a mistake on the slide, not a blank to fill in. A label like "Your number" would read better.
