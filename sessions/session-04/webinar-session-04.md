## The cast
- **The leader: Carla Brennan**, CEO of a regional manufacturer (fictional), back for a fourth time. Since her board said yes, she has been told to "be more visible." Buyers and plant leaders should know how her company thinks. She has no time and hates sounding like a sales bot.
- **The helper:** an AI chat Carla briefs once and uses every weekday. It drafts. She posts.

## The string - seven patterns, in the order the routine uses them
Shown (the first two live on screen, the last one from slide 5):
1. **Onboard, Don't Program** - a five-part briefing, played back before any work.
2. **The Chat Is Not Your Memory** - the routine lives in a record, not the chat. When a rule changes ("always get the post's link"), the record changes.
7. **The AI Operations Manager** - the helper is registered like a new hire, with a feedback field she fills in.

The middle four - talk track only (they happened every morning; we don't run them on camera):
3. **Right Tool, Right Job** - LinkedIn's own search, set to show only posts from the last day, finds the posts. No bot ever touches her account.
4. **Automate the Invisible** - the AI does the invisible work: checks each post is worth a comment, asks for missing links, drafts. The comment is relationship work, so it stays hers.
5. **Embed the Expert** - Carla's own true stories, captured once in one interview as a story bank. The helper picks the story that fits the reader and never invents one. She still rewrites every comment herself. (Swapped in for Own the Thinking, Greg, Nov 27.)
6. **Ghost in the Workflow** - the helper has a written job description, so someone else could pick it up.

Not in this session: The Night Shift / Prepare for the Night Shift (left out - the routine doesn't run overnight).

The loop (Greg's version only): the comments point people to the same patterns the routine is built on.

**One Routine, Seven Patterns**
Carla's board wants her "more visible" on LinkedIn. One prompt and a tool that posts for her get her a public reply under a tough-quarter post: "Did a bot write this?"
In this session, Greg Stellflue builds the fix: brief the AI once, keep the routine in a record, rewrite every draft until it's yours, and register the helper like a new hire. Seven patterns, one routine.
**Measure it yourself:** minutes a day for the routine, before and after, and conversations started. Insert your own savings numbers. LinkedIn says comments posted by automation tools aren't allowed ([LinkedIn, March 13, 2026](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/authentic-content-and-conversations)) - the AI drafts, you post.

**The seven, one line each (print it):**
1. Onboard, Don't Program - brief the AI once, and have it play the briefing back.
2. The Chat Is Not Your Memory - keep the routine in a record, not the chat.
3. Right Tool, Right Job - find the posts with LinkedIn's own search.
4. Automate the Invisible - let the AI check and draft; the comment stays yours.
5. Own the Thinking - rewrite until every sentence is something you'd say.
6. Ghost in the Workflow - write the helper's job down so someone else could run it.
7. The AI Operations Manager - register the helper like a new hire.
