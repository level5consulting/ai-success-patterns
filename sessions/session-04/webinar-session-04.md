## The cast
- **The leader: Carla Brennan**, CEO of a regional manufacturer (fictional), back for a fourth time. Since her board said yes, she has been told to "be more visible." Buyers and plant leaders should know how her company thinks. She has no time and hates sounding like a sales bot.
- **The helper:** an AI chat Carla briefs once and uses every weekday. It drafts. She posts.

## The string (in the order the routine uses them)
Shown live (first two and the last one):
1. **Onboard, Don't Program** - a five-part briefing, played back before any work.
2. **The Chat Is Not Your Memory** - the routine lives in a record, not the chat. When a rule changes ("always get the post's link"), the record changes.
3. **The AI Operations Manager** - the helper is registered like a new hire, with a feedback field she fills in.

Talk track only (they happened; we don't run them on camera):
- **Automate the Invisible** - the AI does the invisible work: the fit check, the drafts, the schedule. The comment is relationship work, so it stays hers.
- **Right Tool, Right Job** - LinkedIn's own search, with the Past 24 hours filter, finds the posts. No bot ever touches her account.
- **Ghost in the Workflow** - the helper has a written job description, so someone else could pick it up.

Not in this session: Own the Thinking (dropped, Greg Nov 12); The Night Shift / Prepare for the Night Shift (left out - the routine doesn't run overnight).

The loop (Greg's version only): the comments point people to the same patterns the routine is built on.

## Talking points

**Pre-roll (about 10 seconds, not counted)**
- "One pattern is a tip. A string of them is a routine. Welcome back to A Ten-Minute Taco 'bout AI." [GREG: your call on this line.]

**Act I - The Leader's Anxiety (0:00-1:00)**
- Carla Brennan runs a regional manufacturer. You don't need the earlier sessions for this one.
- Her board said yes to the AI plan. Then the board chair added one more thing: "You need to be more visible. Buyers should know how we think."
- Visible means LinkedIn. Comment on other people's posts, every day, and sound like a person.
- She tries it on Monday. Forty minutes scrolling. One comment. It says "Great post!" [GREG: a line about what your own LinkedIn feed looks like - work anniversaries, humblebrags, somebody's sourdough.]
- **Button:** "Every day. On top of running a company. And it has to sound like her."

**Act II - The Operator's Grind (1:00-2:30)**
- So she does what most of us would do. One prompt: "Write me five LinkedIn comments." Five come back. Every one of them could go under any post on earth.
- Then she finds a browser extension that will post them for her. Set it, forget it.
- Wednesday. A CFO she'd like to know posts after a tough earnings call: missed numbers, a hard quarter, owning it in public.
- Carla's name is under it: "Exciting times ahead! Thanks for sharing."
- The CFO replies, in public: "Did a bot write this?"
- Carla doesn't have a good answer, because a bot did.
- And it's against the rules: LinkedIn prohibits bots and automated activity on your account. [4 PM] check - re-open the LinkedIn help page in the evidence list and confirm the wording before recording.
- [GREG: your reaction to "Did a bot write this?" - the worst version you've seen in the wild, kept anonymous.]
- **Button (closes the act):** "One prompt, one tool, one shortcut. And her name on every word of it."

**Act III - The Hero Moment (2:30-7:30) - live, on screen**
- "Let's rewind to Monday. Here's what Carla should have done." Slide 3: the string. "Strings, not singles."
- **Say it plainly first:** "No single pattern fixes this. A few ordinary ones do, used together. And none of them posts anything for her. I'll show you the first two and the last one live. The middle ones happened too - I'll tell you how."
- "Every prompt is on the page - the link is below." Switch to the screen.

**Part A - Onboard, Don't Program (about 1:15, live)**
- Paste the Onboard, Don't Program "Use this pattern" text into a fresh AI session, then Carla's briefing (paste-in 1).
- The AI plays back what it understood. Read one line aloud. "This is the part people skip. It's the part that makes it sound like her."
- If it gets something wrong, fix it on camera - that's the pattern working. If it gets everything right, say: "It got it right. Now it'll get it right every morning."

**Part B - The Chat Is Not Your Memory (about 1:00, live)**
- "This chat won't remember tomorrow. So the routine doesn't live here. It lives in a record."
- Ask the AI to write the briefing and today's rules into a short record (paste-in 2). It comes back as a page Carla can save.
- Add one line on camera: "Always get the post's link." "Week one, she lost a post because it came without its link. One line in the record, and the helper has asked for it every day since."
- Open a fresh chat, paste the record, ask "What's your job today?" It answers from the record. Line to land: "New chat, same helper. The memory is the record, not the chat."
- **Recording rule:** if the fresh chat misses the link rule, say: "That's why you read the record back," point at the line, and move on.

**Part C - the middle, as a talk track (about 1:15, slide 4, prepared - not live)**
- Back to slides. Slide 4 shows the three middle patterns as cards and one before-and-after line.
- **Right Tool, Right Job:** "She finds the posts herself, in LinkedIn's own search, filtered to the past 24 hours. The right tool for finding posts is LinkedIn. The AI never touches her account."
- **Automate the Invisible:** "The AI does the invisible work - checks each post is a fit, skips the AI coach, asks for a missing link, drafts. The comment itself is relationship work. That stays hers."
- **Carla's own line:** "Every draft gets one line only she could write." Show the swap (paste-in 3): the AI's line struck through, Carla's line under it - "When ours retired, we had him quote ten real jobs out loud while we recorded. The binder never caught what he said under his breath."
- **Ghost in the Workflow:** "And the record is also the helper's job description. If Carla's assistant has to run it next month, it's all written down."
- Line to land: "All of that happened every morning. None of it is clever on its own."

**Part D - The AI Operations Manager (about 0:50, live or slide 5)**
- Back to the screen (or slide 5 if the take runs long): Carla's list of AI helpers, one row per helper, like a new hire.
- Fill in the row on camera: "LinkedIn helper - what it does: drafts comments, never posts - when it runs: weekdays - who owns it: Carla - Useful this week? Very."
- "If you can't say who owns it, what it's allowed to do, and whether it's useful, you don't have a helper. You have a stray."
- [GREG: your "one time at band camp" line - the helper is the one that keeps you from telling the COBOL story every day.]
- Line to land: "None of these patterns is clever on its own. Together, it's a routine she'll actually keep."
- Timing: intro 0:20 + A 1:15 + B 1:00 + C 1:15 + D 0:50 = 4:40, leaving 0:20 of slack. Part C is the one to trim if the take runs long.
- Editing: record Parts A, B and D twice, keep the better take; cut the AI's typing waits.

**Act IV - The Value Hypothesis (7:30-9:00)**
- Slide 6. A test, not a promise: run the routine for two weeks.
- **The two measures on screen:** "Minutes a day. Conversations started."

  - Conversations: replies and threads under your comments, and profile visits.

## Act III paste-ins (copy and paste live)

**Paste-in 1 - Carla's briefing (after the Onboard, Don't Program prompt)**
```
Here's your briefing. You're my helper for a daily routine.

1. Who I am: Carla Brennan, CEO of a regional manufacturer - custom and standard machined parts. Thirty years in the business. I started on the shop floor.
2. What we're working on: comments on other people's LinkedIn posts, so buyers and plant leaders get to know how we think.
3. Constraints: three comments a day, spaced out, on posts less than a day old. You draft. I post every comment myself - nothing touches my LinkedIn account. I find the posts.
4. Preferences: short. Sound like me. One specific thing from the post and one from my experience. No "great insights." No sales pitch.
5. What good looks like: people who buy parts or run plants - not AI experts or consultants. Success is replies and conversations.

Play back what you understood before we start.
```
