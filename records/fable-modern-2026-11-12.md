## 1. The Napkin (Ten People, One Idea) - 144 words

The operations director at a trucking company asked her nine team leads for one idea each to fix late deliveries.

Last year the ideas had been all over the place. Dev wanted to move the cutoff time. Rosa wanted to fire a carrier. Pete drew a map on a napkin.

This year all nine arrived on time, formatted and confident. Every one recommended "leveraging real-time visibility to proactively optimize routing." She approved it in one meeting. It was the only idea in the room.

In March, the late trucks were still late.

At the holiday party, Pete mentioned that the dock door on the north side sticks when it's cold. He'd known for two years.

"Why didn't you say so?"

"I did. In my first draft."

Now the first draft comes in on a napkin.

And that's why we say: Ten People, One Idea.

## 2. Let Me Get Back to You (The Chat Is Not Your Memory) - 137 words

Theo ran a three-month vendor selection in one long chat. Every call, every score, every decision went in. The AI never forgot a thing, and it never complained.

In week nine, the steering committee asked why they had ruled out the cheapest vendor.

Theo asked the chat. It answered right away, with a clear, confident reason. Nobody had ever said it.

He scrolled for an hour. The real reason was in there somewhere, between a lunch order and a draft email to legal. He never found it.

On Friday he told the committee, "Let me get back to you," which is what you say when you don't know.

On his next project, Theo kept one page: what we decided, why, and who said so. The chat reads it every morning.

And that's why we say: The Chat Is Not Your Memory.

## 3. The Friendly Room (Assemble the Team) - 128 words

The night before her pitch, the founder asked the AI what it thought of her deck.

"Compelling," it said. She asked again with the numbers slide. "Strong." She asked about the pricing. "Well positioned." She went to bed happy.

The next morning, the first investor flipped to slide four and asked, "Who are you taking customers from?"

She didn't have an answer. The rest of the hour was polite.

In the parking lot she opened the chat again and typed, "Be the investor who said no this morning. Then be her customer. Then be the competitor she's worried about."

It took eleven minutes. The room was not friendly. Her next pitch went better.

And that's why we say: Assemble the Team.
