## Renee Calloway (standing seat - who carries this?)

I'm the one who'll be asked to "organize something." So: who carries this pattern? The manager decides the win and says it out loud. Fine. But step 3 - "celebrate in a way those people would actually enjoy" - in a big company, that becomes "Renee, can you set up a lunch?" The page should say the leader does the thanking themselves. The lunch can be booked by anyone; the words can't.

Step 1 creates a tracking job - someone has to notice when you hit it. If AI saves time across a team, who's counting? Usually me, in a spreadsheet nobody asked for. Say the person who set the goal checks it at the check-in they already have - which is what "When to use it" says. Connect the two.

## Marcus Lee

"People leave when they're not recognized" - that's well supported in my experience of exit interviews, but recognition is rarely the only reason, and the page's "some of them leave" is careful enough. I'd keep it as Greg's own observation, not a statistic. Don't add a number unless it's sourced.

Where it can backfire: "say it out loud." Some people hate public praise. Step 3 covers this indirectly ("a way those people would actually enjoy"), but step 2 says "out loud" with no caveat. I'd add: some people want it said privately - ask.

Second backfire: thanking the person whose job the AI made smaller. If a win is "growing without adding people," celebrating that in front of people who are worried about their jobs lands badly. The page should say: make the win about what the people did, not about headcount.

The dinner story works because it's about what they delivered for the customer and about care (new in town). That's the right model. "Half the team wasn't 21" made me stop for a second - it reads as a detail about the team's age; it's fine, but "too young for a beer" might read cleaner.

"Constant pressure wears people down. Celebrating a win is how they recover." I'd soften "how they recover" to "part of how" - recovery also needs time off and a lighter load, and celebrating isn't a substitute for those.

## Priya Natarajan

I'm one person. Does this work for me? Mostly yes - step 1 and step 2 work alone. I never set a milestone for my AI use, and the line "I have no idea what milestones I've passed, because I never set any" is exactly me.

Step 3 assumes a team. For one person, the celebration is for yourself, and for the people who helped - a client who gave you good data, a spouse who covered dinner. One line: "Working alone? It still counts - mark it for yourself, and thank anyone who helped."

The "Why it matters" is all about people on a team. For a solo worker the reason is different: if you never notice the time you got back, you just fill it with more work. That's my real problem.
