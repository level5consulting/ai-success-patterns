**1. The Napkin (Ten People, One Idea) - wizards.** The queen of a small river kingdom asked her nine wizards how to stop the spring floods. Last year their answers had been all over the place. One wanted a wall. One wanted to move the mill. Old Pell drew a map on a napkin. This year, the night before, all nine consulted the Mirror of Answers. **2. I'll Transmit That Shortly (The Chat Is Not Your Memory) - neighboring galaxy.** Last week, in a neighboring galaxy, Captain Theo spent three months choosing a new engine supplier for his freighter - all of it in one endless conversation with the ship's computer. It never forgot a thing, and never complained. In month three, the Fleet Council asked why they had ruled out the cheapest supplier. Theo asked the computer. It answered at once, with a clear, confident reason. Nobody had ever said it. He searched the logs for a day. The real reason was in there somewhere, between a lunch order and a message to the lawyers. He never found it. He told the Council, "I'll transmit that shortly," which is what captains say when they don't know. On his next voyage, Theo kept one page: what we decided, why, and who said so. The computer reads it every morning watch. And that's why we say: The Chat Is Not Your Memory.

She asked about her prices. "Wise." She asked about her rivals. "None worth worrying about." She went to bed happy. The next morning, the Guild master turned to her fourth page and asked, "Whose customers are you taking?" She didn't have an answer. The rest of the hour was polite. On the road home she held up the crystal and said, "Be the merchant who said no this morning. Then be the farmer who'd buy from me. Then be the rival across the bridge." The crystal clouded over and spoke in three voices. None of them were friendly. Her next pitch went better. And that's why we say: Assemble the Team.

## Plain language: Ghost in the Workflow - 8 rewrites
`2026-11-16-plain-ghost` - night of Monday, November 16 - answered Tue Nov 17, 11:39 AM CT

All 8 findings from the Nov 9 review still match the live page (checked Nov 16). Each rewrite is its own question.

**1.** Now: "The person who built your AI workflow understood it. They knew why the prompt was worded that way, what the agent was never supposed to touch, and which outputs needed a second look."
Why: CEO. "Workflow", "prompt" and "agent" all arrive in the opening two sentences, and none is defined.
Rewrite: "The person who set up your AI process understood it. They knew why the instructions were worded that way, what the AI was never supposed to touch, and which results needed a second look."

**2.** Now: "That includes agents running on a schedule or triggered by events, prompts a team reuses, chat workflows built into how a process gets done, and any AI that touches business systems or data."
Why: GM, TA. "Triggered by events" and "chat workflows" are developer phrasing.
Rewrite: "That includes AI that runs on its own on a schedule, instructions a team copies and reuses, chats that are now part of how a job gets done, and any AI that can see or change company data."

**3.** Now: "Store it alongside the agent, prompt, or workflow it describes, wherever the team already keeps shared work, and version it with every change."
Why: HR, GM. "Version it" is a developer verb.
Rewrite: "Keep it next to the AI setup it describes, wherever your team keeps shared files, and save a new dated copy every time it changes."

**4.** Now: "Gartner's own guidance on agent sprawl calls for a central inventory and lifecycle rules."
Why: CEO. "Agent sprawl" and "lifecycle rules" are analyst jargon.
Rewrite: "Gartner says companies need one list of every AI agent they run, and rules for when each one gets reviewed and retired."

**5.** Now: "Security writers call these "orphaned agents" and focus on credentials that outlive their owners."
Why: HR, TA. "Credentials" means logins, but says it less plainly.
Rewrite: "Security writers call these 'orphaned agents' and worry most about logins and passwords that outlive the person who set them up."

**6.** Now: "A human verifies this against the actual system configuration - the AI knows what it was told it can reach, not necessarily what it can."
Why: GM. "System configuration" is IT language.
Rewrite: "Have someone check this against the real settings - the AI knows what it was told it can reach, not what it actually can."

**7.** Now: "An AI workflow's logic sits in its tuning history: the revisions, the rejected outputs, the conversations that shaped it. None of that is visible in the finished agent."
Why: CEO. "Tuning history" is unfamiliar.
Rewrite: "An AI setup's logic sits in how it got that way: the rewrites, the rejected answers, the conversations that shaped it. None of that shows in the finished setup."

**8.** Now: "Stay free to change models. When an AI workflow documents its purpose, inputs, prompts and owner outside any one tool, you can move it to a different model without rebuilding it from memory."
Why: TA, CEO. "Models" is used for "AI tools".
Rewrite: "Stay free to switch AI tools. When the record of what it does, what it uses and who owns it lives outside any one tool, you can move it without rebuilding it from memory."
