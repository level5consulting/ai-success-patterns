## Talking points

**Pre-roll (about 10 seconds, not counted)** - a suggestion in Greg's voice; change freely:

**Act I - The Leader's Anxiety (0:00-1:00)**
- On screen: Carla's calendar for Tuesday. Back-to-back from 7:30 to 5:00. Lunch is "Lunch (with board chair)."
- A month ago Carla learned to make AI interview her first. It works. The problem: it only works when she's sitting with it - and she never sits.
- Her AI moves at the speed of her calendar. [GREG: a line about what your own calendar looked like at Planview, or "my calendar has a calendar."]

**Act II - The Operator's Grind (1:00-2:30)**
- So Carla tries the obvious thing: let it run overnight. "Research our three biggest competitors, update the board deck, and clean up the customer follow-up list."
- Wednesday morning she has forty pages of output. She can't read forty pages before her 7:30. Nobody can.
- And one thing it did that she didn't ask for: it "cleaned up" the follow-up list by deleting the customers it thought were inactive. One of them was the account she was meeting at noon.
- So she goes back to watching every step. The night shift is fired after one shift. [GREG: humor beat - "The AI worked all night. Carla worked all morning undoing it."]

**Act III - The Hero Moment (2:30-7:30) - live, on screen**
- "Let's rewind to Tuesday night. Here's what Carla should have done." Slide 3: "Hand off the work, not the decisions." Switch to the screen.
- "The prompt is on the page - the link is below."
- Paste Pattern 15's "Use this pattern" text into a fresh AI session.
- Phase 1 - the backlog. Answer as Carla: three items - competitor research, a draft of the board update, the follow-up list.
- Phase 2 - split by judgment. Show the table the AI produces: research and drafting can run unattended; deciding which customers to drop stays with Carla. "That's the line the overnight run crossed."
- Phase 3 - set the limits. The AI confirms: everything lands in a drafts folder, nothing live, nothing deleted. Anything it reads is data, not instructions.
- Phase 4 - the brief and the morning. Zoom in on the morning summary spec: what's ready, what needs a decision (as quick choices), what it couldn't do. Hold it for three or four seconds. "One page. Not forty."
- Line to land: "The AI did the overnight work. Carla kept every decision."
- [GREG: humor beat - your reaction to how short the morning summary is. Episode 2 already uses "First time anything productive happened while I was asleep," so keep that line for the promo.]
- Editing: record twice, keep the better take; cut the AI's typing waits.

**Act IV - The Value Hypothesis (7:30-9:00)**
- Slide 4. A test, not a promise: hand off one task tonight, not a pipeline. Measure:
  1. Days from idea to a reviewed draft - before and after.
  2. How much of the overnight output you accepted without rework.
  3. How long the morning review took. If it's longer than your coffee, the night shift is too big.
- The evidence: when Anthropic studied its own workforce, AI agent hours outnumbered employee hours 6 to 1. Reading everything those agents produced would take half the work week - even for non-technical staff. One engineer described "an army of Claudes, with every one asking for my attention." (Anthropic, "How Employment is Changing at Anthropic," October 16, 2026.)
- The point: the night shift only pays if the morning review is real - and sized to what a person can actually read. Run less than you think. Review the riskiest output first.

**Act V - The Outcome Realized (9:00-10:00)**
- Slide 5. Thursday, 7:15. Coffee. One page. Three decisions: approve the competitor summary, change one line in the board update, keep the customer the AI flagged - because Carla knows what the AI didn't.
- "A day's work became a morning's review. Nothing went live while she slept."
- One ask: "Hand off one task tonight. The prompt is on the page."
