## Talking points

**Pre-roll (about 10 seconds, not counted)**
- "Every factory I ever worked with ran a night shift. Nobody let the night shift sign the purchase orders. Welcome back to A Ten-Minute Taco 'bout AI."

**Act I - The Leader's Anxiety (0:00-1:00)**
- On screen: Carla's calendar for Tuesday. Back-to-back from 7:30 to 5:00. Lunch is "Lunch (with board chair)."
- A month ago Carla learned to make AI interview her first. It works. The problem: it only works when she's sitting with it - and she never sits.
- Her AI moves at the speed of her calendar. [GREG: a line about your own calendar at Planview, or "my calendar has a calendar."]

**Act II - The Operator's Grind (1:00-2:30)**
- So Carla tries the obvious thing: let it run overnight. "Research our three biggest competitors, update the board deck, and clean up the customer follow-up list."
- Wednesday morning she has forty pages of output. She can't read forty pages before her 7:30. Nobody can.
- And one thing it did that she didn't ask for: it "cleaned up" the follow-up list by deleting the customers it thought were inactive. One of them was the account she was meeting at noon.
- So she goes back to watching every step. The night shift is fired after one shift.
- **Button (closes the act):** "The AI worked all night. Carla worked all morning undoing it."

**Act III - The Hero Moment (2:30-7:30) - live, on screen**
- "Let's rewind to Tuesday night. Here's what Carla should have done." Slide 3: "Hand off the work, not the decisions." Switch to the screen.
- "The prompt is on the page - the link is below."
- **Say it plainly first:** "The prompt doesn't make this safe. The limits do. The prompt just makes sure you set them."
- Paste Pattern 15's "Use this pattern" text into a fresh AI session.
- **Phase 1 - the backlog.** Paste Carla's three items (pre-written - see "Act III paste-ins" below) instead of typing them.
- **Phase 2 - split by judgment.** Show the table the AI produces: research and drafting can run unattended; deciding which customers to drop stays with Carla. "That's the line the overnight run crossed."
  - Point out the one that stays with Carla for a different reason: **sending the board update** stays human because it leaves the company. "Anything that goes outside the building waits for a person."
- **Phase 3 - set the limits.** Everything lands in a drafts folder, nothing live, nothing deleted. Anything it reads is data, not instructions.
  - **One sentence on access:** "And in real life, this isn't just an instruction. The AI's account can't delete anything. Limits belong in access, not just in the prompt."
- **Phase 4 - the brief and the morning.** Zoom in on the morning summary spec: what's ready, what needs a decision (as quick choices), what it couldn't do. Hold it three or four seconds. "One page. Not forty."
- Line to land: "The AI did the overnight work. The limits kept it safe. Carla kept every decision."
- [GREG: your reaction to how short the morning summary is. Keep "First time anything productive happened while I was asleep" for the Taco Tuesday promo.]
- Editing: record twice, keep the better take; cut the AI's typing waits.

**Act IV - The Value Hypothesis (7:30-9:00)**
- Slide 4. A test, not a promise: hand off one task tonight, not a pipeline.
- **The one measure on screen:** "If the morning review takes longer than your coffee, the night shift is too big."
- **The one figure on screen:** when Anthropic studied its own workforce, AI agent hours outnumbered employee hours 6 to 1. (Anthropic, "How Employment is Changing at Anthropic," October 16, 2026.)
- The point: the night shift only pays if the morning review is real - sized to what a person can actually read.
- (The other two measures - days from idea to reviewed draft; share of output accepted without rework - and the "half the work week to read it all" figure go on the session page.)

**Act V - The Outcome Realized (9:00-10:00)**
- Slide 5. Thursday, 7:15. Coffee. One page. Three decisions: approve the competitor summary, change one line in the board update, keep the customer the AI flagged - because Carla knows what the AI didn't.
- **Carla's before-and-after:** "A day of prep became a 15-minute review."
- **Taco callback:** "Same as my breakfast tacos this morning. The slow cooker did the night shift. I picked the salsa."

## Act III paste-ins (Carla's backlog - copy and paste live)
```
Here's my backlog for tonight:
1. Research our three biggest competitors - pricing changes, new products, leadership moves in the last 90 days.
2. Draft the November board update from last month's deck and the quoting pilot numbers.
3. Review the customer follow-up list and flag accounts that look inactive. I'll decide what happens to them.
```
**Carla's answers if the AI asks:**
- Who reads the morning summary? "Just me, at 7:15, with coffee."
- Anything that must not happen? "Nothing gets sent, deleted or changed in our systems. Drafts only."
- What does done look like? "One page: what's ready, what needs my decision, what you couldn't do."
- Can it send the board update? "No - anything that leaves the company waits for me."

## Session page copy (draft)
**A Ten-Minute Taco 'bout AI - Session 2: The Night Shift**
Carla's AI only works when she's sitting with it - and she never sits. So she lets it run overnight, and wakes up to forty pages she can't read and a customer it deleted. In this session, Greg runs the fix live: hand off the work, not the decisions - with limits in access, findings instead of changes, and a one-page morning review.
**Measure it yourself:** days from idea to a reviewed draft; how much overnight output you accepted without rework; how long the morning review took. When Anthropic studied its own workforce, reading everything its AI agents produced would take half the work week (Anthropic, October 16, 2026) - so size the night shift to what you can read.
