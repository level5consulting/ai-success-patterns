## Round 1 - Problem and Value

**Rachel (adversarial):** Every major AI tool now has projects, memory, or uploaded files that persist. Your Problem reads like it's 2023. If you don't say what those features do and don't do, I'll dismiss the page in one line: "just use Projects." Name them, credit them, and say the gap - they decide what to keep, they don't keep the reason behind a decision, and they don't travel when you switch tools.

**Linda:** You named GitHub. Fine - but the first example I hit is a repository, and I'm out. Lead with the version I'd use: one shared document with four headings. Put GitHub second, as what the library uses.

**Omar:** The October 15 story is good - it's honest. But it's long for the Problem, and it's about building a website. Mine is five client chats where I've given the same correction three times. Tighten yours to three sentences and add one line about the everyday version.

**Victor:** The Value says "anyone can pick it up." That's my whole life. Make it concrete: when someone leaves, their AI work is either a record I can read, or it's gone. That's the line that would make a manager share this page.

**Round 1 proposed changes:**
1. Name the built-in features (projects, memory, uploaded files), say what they do well, and the gap: they choose what to keep, don't keep the reasons, and don't move with you between tools. (Rachel)
2. Lead the "one home" with a shared document; mention GitHub second, as what this library uses. (Linda)
3. Tighten the October 15 story to three sentences and add an everyday example (the same correction given three times). (Omar)
4. Make "anyone can pick it up" concrete: when someone leaves, their AI work is a record you can read, or it's gone. (Victor)
