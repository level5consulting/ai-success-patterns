# Rant Thursday "The AI Eye Chart" - Assemble the Team (November 11, 2026, overnight)

**The work, in one sentence:** a Rant Thursday post for November 12 that sets our own overstuffed cheat-sheet parody against an invented eye chart (five Start-here names on the big top line, made-up tips blurring below), with the one-page Start here card, tied to this week's theme, Assemble the Team.

Greg was asleep, so the team was proposed and run in one pass; the team itself is up for his OK in the form. All four reviewers are fictional. They reviewed draft 1 (rant-draft.md) and all three images. Findings only - nothing below has been applied to the clean copy.

## The team

1. **Dale Whitcomb (adversarial)** - an independent AI trainer with a big LinkedIn following who posts his own cheat sheets and carousels; beginners thank him for them every week. He is in the room because he thinks this rant is a consultant punching down at other creators to sell services, and that cheat sheets do real good for people starting out.
2. **Marisol Vega** - HR director at a 300-person distribution company; not technical, reads LinkedIn on her phone between meetings. She is in the room because she is the reader who scrolls past anything she has to work to understand.
3. **Ken Ostrowski** - PMO director at a regional health system who has run steering committees for twelve years. He is in the room because he is the buyer who would actually say "did you run Assemble the Team?" in a meeting, and he checks whether the theme tie is real or bolted on.
4. **Bree Lindqvist** - brand designer who builds LinkedIn carousels and post images for small firms. She is in the room for the visuals: what reads at phone size, image order, and whether the joke lands at thumbnail.

## The voices

**Dale (adversarial):** "Two hundred tips on one page. Ninety-nine shortcuts to memorize. Nine colors." You didn't name anybody, but anyone who saw those two posts knows exactly which ones you mean, and so do the people who made them. That's naming with extra steps. And here's my real objection: my cheat sheets get thanked by people who were scared to open the tool at all. For them a page of tips is the first step, not the problem. You're a consultant with a services page telling people the free thing they found is wrong. If you don't say once that a cheat sheet can help someone get started, this reads as sour grapes. Also line 10 of your eye chart says "memorize these shortcuts" - that's a wink at one specific post. And put "all tips made up" on the chart somewhere, or the first comment will be someone saying "tip 37 is mine."

**Marisol:** I got it by the eye chart - that's the moment it clicked. Before that, image 1 confused me. At phone size it's just a wall, and for a second I thought you were selling it. Tell me it's a joke about your own site before I look at it. "YAANOWR" stopped me cold - I tried to pronounce it. Put the meaning first, then the letters. "Steering committee" is fine for me. The link at the end says "five names, one line each" - I'd click faster if I knew it was free and short.

**Ken:** The common-language point is the best thing in it - "did you run Assemble the Team on that" is exactly how it would sound in my meetings. But the Assemble the Team tie comes in the last paragraph and it's thin. Give me the real version: the business case goes to the steering committee Thursday, so on Tuesday you name the CFO, the skeptical sponsor and the delivery lead and hear them first. And "the team was not gentle" is a tease - tell me one thing they caught. You also say "each one sets up the next" but never show it; one sentence on how the five run in a row would carry the strings point.

**Bree:** The eye chart reads at thumbnail; the parody doesn't. LinkedIn shows the first image biggest, so if the eye chart leads, people get the joke before they tap. The parody is the better punch line second. On the chart itself, "Say it out loud" five times is noise - once, beside the top block, is enough. The blur works; the bottom line is properly unreadable. On the card: three images makes the post a carousel-ish tap-through; the Start here card would do more work as the first comment with the link, so the post stays two pictures and one idea.
