## Renee Calloway (adversarial)

I'll say the uncomfortable part first. Every one of these five patterns is written for the person who starts the work. None of them is written for the person who ends up holding it, and in most companies that's me.

Avoid AI Slop is fine on its face - the expert answers the interview, so the expert carries it. But in real life the director doesn't sit for a 10-question interview. They say "Renee, can you get the AI to draft the steering update?" Now I'm the one answering questions about a project I coordinate but don't own. The page says "if the interview comes up empty, you're not ready to write yet." Who tells the director that? Me. The page should say plainly: the person who owns the opinions sits for the interview. Don't delegate the interview.

The Chat Is Not Your Memory: "Start: read the record. End: update the record." Whose job is "end: update the record" on a team? It's the coordinator's. That's a new recurring task with no name on it. The page says "one home, not five" - good - but it never says one owner. I've kept that document before. When it's nobody's job, it's my job, unpaid.

The Night Shift is the one that worries me. "A day's work becomes a morning's review." For one person, sure. On a team, the morning review lands on whoever is in first and knows where everything lives. And "build the backlog first - write each item as the brief" is real work. The page counts the hours saved overnight and never counts the hours of writing briefs.

Assemble the Team: cheap for the person running it. But "record your calls" - again, a record someone keeps.

Ghost in the Workflow is the one that's most honest - it says "a named owner." But it doesn't say what happens when the named owner is the coordinator who didn't build it. Inheriting someone's AI setup with a nice record is still inheriting it.

My objection to the whole premise: these patterns can quietly become another layer of invisible work routed to the people who already carry the invisible work. If the library doesn't name that, it's part of the problem Christina described. I'm not asking you to drop them. I'm asking each page to say who carries it and what they stop doing.

## Luis Ortega

I'm the one who checks. My team of eight uses this stuff, and when something goes out wrong, it's my name.

Avoid AI Slop actually takes work off my plate - when someone drafts from their own answers, I'm not rewriting generic text at 6 PM. I'd say that on the page: the reviewer spends less time fixing voice.

The Night Shift: if I ran this for my team, the morning review is mine. Eight people's overnight output reviewed by one lead is not "minutes." The page says "start small - one task the first night." Good. I'd add a limit: the morning review has to fit the reviewer's morning, and if it doesn't, cut what runs at night. Greg tracks his own review time - that's the proof, it should be in the pattern.

Assemble the Team takes something off my plate: fewer surprises after I send a deck up. I'd want the page to say who runs it - the author, before it reaches the lead - not the lead as an extra step.

Chat Is Not Your Memory: the record saves me from re-deciding things. I'd say that as the trade: ten minutes updating the record versus the meeting where we argue about what we decided.

## Janet Pruitt

From where I sit, the risk is not the patterns. It's the pattern being adopted by a manager who adds it on top of everything and takes nothing away.

None of the five pages has a line like "what you stop doing." Avoid AI Slop could say: stop line-editing AI drafts. Chat Is Not Your Memory: stop re-briefing in every new chat. Night Shift: stop the evening prep. Assemble the Team: fewer rework rounds after review. Ghost: stop the panic rebuild when someone leaves. That's the "comes off their plate" half, and right now it's implied, not said.

The Night Shift's language worries me a little - "work moves while you're away." For an individual, that's a gift. Read by a manager, it can sound like "work should move all the time." I'd add one line that the night shift is the AI's shift, not yours - nobody should be checking it at midnight.

Ghost in the Workflow says someone leaves and the record survives. Good. But the named owner is a person with a workload. When an AI asset gets an owner, something else should come off that person.

## Ken Albright (CFO)

I read every Value section. They count hours saved and give a pay figure. Fine. But a CFO asks for the net, not the gross.

The Night Shift is the clearest example: hours saved overnight, but zero hours counted for writing the backlog briefs and the morning review. The page should say "count the review time and the brief-writing time too," and say the number that matters is what's left.

The Chat Is Not Your Memory and Ghost in the Workflow both create recurring upkeep. Upkeep is a cost line. Put it in the Value section next to the savings: "budget ten minutes at the end of each session" or similar - your own number.

Assemble the Team: cheap, I have no cost objection. Avoid AI Slop: the interview takes the expert's time, which is the most expensive time in the building. The value line should say that honestly - you trade some expert time up front for less rewrite time later.

What comes off the plate is where I'd lead. If I can't see what someone stops doing, I assume they're doing both.
