# Red team - The Expertise Bridge (November 10-11, 2026)

Findings only - nothing applied. Greg decides each item in the Morning Review form (Accept / Reject / Accept with edits). Reviewed: patterns/28-the-expertise-bridge.md (draft 1.1: level Practice, category Accuracy, set November 10). Pattern run: Assemble the Team.

**Team (proposed in draft 1, approved by Greg November 10, 2026). All four are fictional reviewers:**
- **Diane Kessler (adversarial)** - CFO who has been handed half-baked AI finance work: does the pattern prevent polished wrong numbers, or just polish them?
- **Marcus Bell** - HR director who has never built a budget case: is it usable in an afternoon?
- **Anita Rao** - senior finance partner who fears being bypassed: does it respect the expert and save her time?
- **Gordon Pike** - compliance and audit lead: regulated work, company data, and who reviewed what.

---

## Round 1 - Problem, Value and Context

**Diane (adversarial):** The Problem is my week. "The CFO finds the hole in the first two minutes" - yes. But read the Solution as the person who sent me that work. Step 3 makes her mark assumptions, Step 5 has the AI grill her, Step 6 packages it neatly. Everything gets more polished. The only thing that stops a wrong number reaching me is Step 7, and Step 7 is the step people skip on a Thursday afternoon with a Friday deadline. "The rule that doesn't bend" is a sentence in Notes. It's not a mechanism. If the expert isn't available before the deadline, what does the draft say about itself when it lands on my desk? Right now, nothing. **Verdict: right instinct, but as written it could make wrong work harder to spot. Give the draft a visible "not yet reviewed" status that only the expert can clear.**

**Marcus:** This is me. I could do Steps 1 to 4 in an afternoon, and the prompt is what I'd actually paste. The term list in Step 2 is the part I've been missing - I didn't know "run rate" and "budget year" were different questions. Two things. First, I don't know who my reviewer is on day one; Phase 1 says "help them name one," good, but Step 1 should also tell me to ask that person for a past case that got approved. That would save me half the afternoon. Second, the AI playing "a skeptical finance partner" - how do I know it's asking my company's questions and not generic ones? I don't. **Verdict: usable in an afternoon. Tell me to get a real example early, and be honest that the rehearsal is generic.**

**Anita:** The Notes are on my side - "use their time well, not bypass them" - but the Value section isn't. "Stands in for time you'd otherwise need from a finance partner" reads, to a finance partner, like "you won't need finance." What it really saves is me teaching basics. Say that. And "Experts generally prefer a prepared colleague" - probably true of me, but it's stated as a general fact with nothing behind it. Either source it or turn it into advice: ask your expert how they want it. On "tell the expert AI helped": keeping it as a step is right, but "AI helped" tells me nothing about where to look. Tell me what it did - explained terms, drafted the narrative, suggested numbers - and I know which parts to check hardest. A day ahead is fine for a short package; three open questions is exactly right. **Verdict: respects the expert in intent; fix the Value wording and make the disclosure specific, and it saves me real time.**

**Gordon:** The "does not replace" list is good but it stops at tax, legal, audited numbers and contracts. Your own worked example puts salary bands and hiring data into an AI. That's employee pay data. Before any of it goes in, the reader needs to know which AI tools the company approves for that kind of data, and to leave out names and individual pay. You have a pattern for this - Avoid the Naughty List - and it isn't connected. Second, "who reviewed what": Step 6 keeps the draft, sources, assumptions and open questions, but not the review itself. When I audit this in a year I need the reviewer's name, the date, what they checked, and what changed. Third, readers won't always know whether something is regulated. Say: if you're not sure, treat it as regulated and ask. **Verdict: sound line on regulated work, but silent on company data and no review trail. Both are fixable in a sentence or two.**

**Round 1 proposed changes:** see 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 10 below.

## Round 2 - Solution and Use this pattern

**Diane (adversarial):** Step 5 is where I'd worry most. A reader who survives an AI "skeptical finance partner" will walk into my office thinking they've already passed finance. Say plainly that a good rehearsal is not a pass - the AI doesn't know our numbers, our history or what I'm trading this request against.

**Marcus:** Step 5 is one skeptic. This week is Assemble the Team, and the case actually has to get past more than one person: the finance partner, the CFO who has to fund it instead of something else, and the hiring managers who'll live with the result. Let me rehearse with three voices. That's the version I'd actually trust.

**Anita:** Phase 6 should produce the disclosure line with specifics, not "one line saying AI helped prepare it." And put the reviewer status at the top of the package, not the bottom.

**Gordon:** Phase 1 should ask, before anything else, whether the work uses company or employee data and whether this AI tool is approved for it. The prompt's regulated rule should include "if unsure, treat it as regulated."

**Round 2 proposed changes:** see 1, 3, 6 and 7 below.

## Round 3 - Fit with Greg's priorities and housekeeping

**Strings, not singles (flag):** The string is stated well in Notes ("learn it, draft it, rehearse it, write it down, hand it over") and four patterns are named in Steps 3, 5, 6 and 7. But a reader who stops at the lead sees no string. Avoid the Naughty List is missing from the string and from Connections (Gordon).

**This week's theme, Assemble the Team (flag):** Present as Step 5, but run as one skeptical voice. Change 1 makes it a real team rehearsal.

**Value realized (flag):** No dollar figures, no statistics, readers insert their own savings - consistent. No "hires you don't need" wording on the reader-facing text (it appears only in the internal Sources line as a file name). Anita's change 5 tightens the role wording so it reads as saving the expert's time, not replacing the expert.

**Writing rules:** No em dashes found. No pattern numbers in reader-facing text (line 3 is internal). "Context" appears only as the site's section heading, not for what you give the AI. One unsourced general claim: "Experts generally prefer a prepared colleague" (change 8). The worked example's "thirty minutes instead of two meetings" is inside a labelled made-up example - fine as is.

## Proposed changes

1. Step 5 and prompt Phase 5: rehearse with a small team instead of one skeptic - a skeptical finance partner, the CFO who has to fund it over something else, and the people who will live with the result - so the pattern actually runs Assemble the Team. (Marcus; theme flag)
2. Step 6 and prompt Phase 6: put a status line at the top of every draft and package - "Not yet reviewed by [name]" until the expert clears it - so an unreviewed draft can never pass as finished, even on deadline. (Diane, adversarial)
3. Step 5: add "A good rehearsal is not a pass - the AI doesn't know your company's numbers, history or priorities, and its questions are generic until your expert asks the real ones." (Diane, adversarial; Marcus)
4. Step 1: ask your named reviewer early for a past approved example or their template, and tell them the review is coming. (Marcus; Anita)
5. Value: replace "stands in for time you'd otherwise need from a finance partner" with "stands in for the basics you'd otherwise ask a finance partner to teach you," so it reads as saving the expert's time, not replacing the expert. (Anita)
6. Step 7 and prompt Phase 6: say what the AI did, not just that it helped - which terms it explained, which parts it drafted, which numbers are assumptions - so the expert knows where to look hardest. (Anita; Gordon)
7. Step 3 and prompt Phase 1: before company or employee data goes in, check the AI tool is approved for it and leave out names and individual pay, and add Avoid the Naughty List to the string and Connections. (Gordon; strings flag)
8. Notes, "Respect the expert": replace "Experts generally prefer a prepared colleague with a short list of sharp questions" with advice - "Ask your expert how and when they want it sent" - since the general claim has no source. (Anita)
9. Lead: end with the string in plain words - "Learn it, draft it, rehearse it, write it down, hand it over." (Strings flag; Marcus)
10. Context, Step 7 and the prompt's regulated rule: add employee pay and personal data to the "does not replace" list, add "if you're not sure whether it's regulated, treat it as regulated and ask," and record the review itself - reviewer, date, what they checked, what changed. (Gordon)
