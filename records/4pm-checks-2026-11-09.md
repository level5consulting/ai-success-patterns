## 4. Models Are Not Good at Everything - weak spots with evidence

Beyond math, copying exact data and regulatory work, two more that have evidence (propose them in the draft; Greg decides):
- **Made-up citations and quotes.** Courts keep getting filings with AI-invented cases. Damien Charlotin's AI Hallucination Cases database tracks them; a Stanford CIS analysis (October 2025) of 114 US cases with attorneys found about 90% came from solo or small firms. Fix: open every source and check the quote yourself (ties to Research Partner, Not All-Knowing Oracle). https://cyberlaw.stanford.edu/blog/2025/10/whos-submitting-ai-tainted-filings-in-court/
- **Long documents - the middle gets missed.** Liu et al., "Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts" (arXiv 2307.03172, TACL 2024): models do best when the key information is at the start or end and much worse when it's in the middle, even long-context models. Fix: ask about one section at a time, or put the key material first. https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172

Regulatory section: no new source needed - the fix is "check the official source and have a qualified person sign off."
