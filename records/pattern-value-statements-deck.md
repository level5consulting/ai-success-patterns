## Slide 2 - How to read these slides

- **Insert your own savings numbers.** Every dollar figure here is an example. Yours are the ones that count.
- **Count hours first.** The hours you'd spend doing the work, booking it, or chasing it, times your own pay rates.
- **Pay only, not benefits.** The figures are median annual pay. Benefits add more; we don't have a verified figure for that, so we leave it out.
- **One row is never a total.** Each pattern lists the kinds of roles whose work it covers. Don't add them up. Nobody books all of them at once.
- **It is a hire you did not have to make, a contractor you did not need.** It lets you run with the lean team you already have. Some patterns honestly cover no role at all, and those slides say so.
- **Source:** U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook, May 2025 median annual pay (bls.gov/ooh).

## Slide 30 - For a small business: count it in hours

No salaries here. Most small businesses never hired an editor or a financial analyst, and that's the point: the owner does the work. So count your own hours.

- **Avoid AI Slop:** a few hours of your own time on a client proposal that still sounds like you.
- **Assemble the Team:** an hour or two of second-guessing a price change before you send it to your best client.
- **The Chat Is Not Your Memory:** the time you spend re-explaining your business to the AI every time you open a new chat.
- **The Night Shift:** the evening you'd spend pulling together tomorrow's prep.
- **Automate the Invisible:** the admin hours (booking, confirmations, follow-ups) that don't get done until Sunday night.

These are examples, not measurements. Your hours x what an hour of your time is worth = your numbers.
