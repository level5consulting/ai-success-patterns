## Round 1 - Problem and Value

**Hank (adversarial):** I've sat through this exact slide five times: five boxes, an arrow, "you are here." Every one of those programs died, and not because nobody knew the stage. They died because nobody owned the result. Your Problem says "the failure is not knowing which stage you're in." I'd say the failure is that nobody is accountable for moving. If this pattern doesn't put a name next to each team's next step, it's one more maturity model - and your own Context says it isn't one. Also, "Returning value" as a stage name lets people off the hook: value isn't a stage you arrive at, it's the test of every stage.

**Priya:** The research paragraph is a good addition, but it's slightly loose. Rogers is about how adoption spreads across a population over time - the early adopters to laggards curve. CBAM's Stages of Concern are about what an individual worries about as they adopt. They support different claims: Rogers supports "people start at different times"; CBAM supports "people need different help at different stages." Say which supports which. And note your draft stages mix the two: "Anxious" is a concern (CBAM's Personal stage), "Integrating" is a behavior. That's fine, but be deliberate. One more thing - the page never says how long a stage lasts. People will ask.

**Tom:** The messy middle paragraph is my life right now. But Value says "Leaders can tell normal progress from a real problem." How? Give me the tell. What does normal messy-middle look like versus a program that's actually failing? That's the sentence I'd take to my CIO. Your own example - all day per pattern before, a couple of hours after - is great, and it should be in Value too, not just the Problem: it's proof that moving a stage changes the economics.

**Lena:** "Fewer people get left behind - or held back" is the line I care about. On my team, the power users get bored waiting for the training to catch up, and the anxious ones get overwhelmed by what the power users are doing. The Value section should say the pattern lets one team run two speeds at once. Right now it reads like it's about whole teams being at one stage.

**Round 1 proposed changes:**
1. Add accountability: each team's next step has a named owner and a date - or it's slideware. (Hank)
2. Reconsider the last stage name, "Returning value," since value is the test at every stage; alternatives: "Scaling," "Compounding," "Leading." (Hank)
3. Tighten the research paragraph: Rogers supports "people start at different times"; CBAM supports "people need different help at each stage." (Priya)
4. Add a line on how long a stage typically takes - or say plainly that it varies and what drives it. (Priya)
5. Add the tell for a healthy messy middle vs. a failing program (e.g., usage spreading and early wins repeating vs. usage flat and the same few people doing everything). (Tom)
6. Repeat Greg's all-day-to-two-hours example in Value as proof that moving a stage changes the economics. (Tom)
7. Make Value explicit that one team can run at two speeds - individuals, not just teams, sit at different stages. (Lena)

## Round 2 - Context and Solution (including "Use this pattern")

**Hank (adversarial):** Step 3 hands each stage a next step - good - but "Anxious teams need safety and one small win" is soft. Who delivers the win, and by when? Step 5, "say so up front," is the best line on the page, because it's the one that saves the program from being cancelled. Make it Step 1. If leadership hasn't agreed the messy middle is coming, nothing else matters.

**Priya:** Step 2, "place each team honestly," needs a method. "A short survey, a conversation, or a look at what people use" is three methods and no guidance. Greg's GO Framework already has a five-dimension Personal AI Readiness Assessment - point to it here, or give three questions. And the "Use this pattern" prompt asks the AI to place the group on stages after a few questions; tell it to say how confident it is, since it's working from one person's description.

**Tom:** Step 4 - "measure each stage by its own yardstick" - is the thing I'll use. Give me the yardstick in a small table: stage, what you measure, what good looks like. That's the artifact I'd put in front of my steering committee. And link Pattern 13 there: invisible work is often where a team in the Practicing stage finds its first real value.

**Lena:** The pattern says "Practicing teams need to share what works (Pattern 08)." On my team the sharing happens person to person, not through a pattern. Suggest pairing: put a power user with an anxious user for one task a week. That's the cheapest way I've found to move people a stage. Also, the "Use this pattern" prompt is good, but it asks about a whole group - let it also be run by one person about themselves.

**Round 2 proposed changes:**
8. Move "Expect the messy middle - and say so up front" to Step 1. (Hank)
9. Make Step 3's next steps concrete - who delivers, by when. (Hank; ties to change 1)
10. Give Step 2 a method: point to the GO Framework's Personal AI Readiness Assessment, or three placement questions. (Priya)
11. In "Use this pattern," have the AI state its confidence in the placement, since it's based on one person's description. (Priya)
12. Turn Step 4 into a small table: stage, what to measure, what good looks like. (Tom)
13. Link Pattern 13, Automate the Invisible, as a source of first value for Practicing teams. (Tom)
14. Add pairing - a power user with an anxious user for one task a week - as a next step for Anxious and Experimenting people. (Lena)
15. Let "Use this pattern" run for an individual as well as a group. (Lena)
