## Round 1 - Problem and Value

**Natalie (adversarial):** I spent a year getting everyone onto one tool and one template. Consistent proposals are easier to compare and approve. Your Problem reads like consistency is the enemy. Say which sameness is the problem - same thinking - and which is fine - same format. Otherwise I'll read this as an attack on standards.

**Kenji:** The competitor point is the one my CEO would care about, and it's one sentence in paragraph three. Lead the business case with it: if your strategy comes from the same model as your competitors', it's their strategy too.

**Amara:** The Problem talks about "different backgrounds and ways of reasoning" getting sanded off. That's true - but say who loses most. People writing in a second language, or from a culture that argues differently, are the ones whose ideas get polished into the default. It's also why they're the ones most tempted to let AI rewrite them.

**Luis:** Greg's travel agent example is the best thing here - but it's about images, and the study is about writing. Show the same move in a meeting: ten people, ideas written down first, then AI. That's the example I'd copy on Monday.

**Round 1 proposed changes:**
1. Say plainly that the problem is same thinking, not same format - consistent templates are fine. (Natalie)
2. Move the competitor point up: if your strategy comes from the same model as your competitors', it isn't a strategy. (Kenji)
3. Name who loses most: people writing in a second language or from a different tradition - their ideas get polished into the default first. (Amara)
4. Add a short meeting example next to the travel agent one: ideas written down first, then AI, and what changed. (Luis)

## Round 2 - Context, Solution, and Use this pattern

**Natalie (adversarial):** Step 1 - "each person writes down their own idea, no AI" - sounds like a rule I'd have to enforce in every meeting. Make it lightweight: five minutes at the start, not a policy.

**Kenji:** Step 2, "ask AI for range," is right, but the prompt needs teeth. Ask it for the option your competitors would never choose, and the one your own team would reject first. Those are the useful ones.

**Amara:** Step 4, "keep the words that carry meaning" - give people permission explicitly. Many will assume the AI version is "more professional" and therefore better. Say: if the original says it better for your audience, the original wins.

**Luis:** The measure - "count the genuinely different options" - is good. Add a before/after: run one brainstorm the usual way and one with ideas first, and compare how many different options reached the decision.

**Natalie (adversarial), on Use this pattern:** Phase 1 refuses to suggest anything until the user writes their view. Some users will just want help. Let them skip it - but have the AI say once why it asked first.

**Round 2 proposed changes:**
5. Step 1: make it light - five quiet minutes at the start of a session, not a policy. (Natalie)
6. Step 2: ask AI for the option your competitors would never choose and the one your team would reject first. (Kenji)
7. Step 4: if the original says it better for your audience, the original wins - say so explicitly. (Amara)
8. Measure: one brainstorm the usual way, one with ideas first; compare how many different options reached the decision. (Luis)
9. Use this pattern, Phase 1: the user can skip giving their own view, but the AI explains once why it asks first. (Natalie)
