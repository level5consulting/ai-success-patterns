## Round 1 - Problem and Value

**Gary (adversarial):** I've funded a lot of "we'll figure it out as we go." It's how projects run forever. Your Problem names two failures - no destination, or a frozen one - but the one I see most is a third: endless iteration with a destination that changes every week and never locks. Your pattern could be read as permission for that. Say up front that iterating has a limit.

**Anita:** "Fund the research step first" is the best thing on the page and it's buried in Notes. It's how my world already works - a spike or a discovery phase, time-boxed, ending in a decision. Put it in the Problem or the Context so a skeptic like Gary recognizes the discipline.

**Paul:** "Sponsors see progress, not churn" is the value I'd pay for. But tell me what I actually get to look at. A one-page "destination, version 3, here's what changed and why" is something I can approve in two minutes. Say that's the artifact.

**Mei:** The measure - "rounds until the destination stops changing" - is good, but it rewards locking early. Pair it with something that catches a bad lock: how often the locked destination had to be reopened later.

**Round 1 proposed changes:**
1. Add the third failure to the Problem: iterating with no limit - the destination keeps moving and never locks. (Gary)
2. Move "fund the research step first" into Context as the familiar version of this discipline. (Anita)
3. In Value, name the artifact the sponsor sees: a one-page destination with its version history. (Paul)
4. Add a second measure: how often a locked destination had to be reopened. (Mei)

## Round 2 - Context, Solution, and Use this pattern

**Gary (adversarial):** Step 5 says lock it "when the destination stops changing for a round." That could take forever. Give it a box - a set number of rounds or a date - and if it isn't locked by then, someone decides. That's the only version I'd fund.

**Anita:** Step 1 uses Pattern 01's four parts. Good. But add who has to agree with the destination. In my world a destination the business owner hasn't seen isn't a destination - it's a guess the team made alone.

**Mei:** Step 2, "ask for options, not an answer," is right. But AI's three options are often the same idea three ways. Tell people to ask for options that differ on one thing that matters - audience, format, or approach - so the comparison teaches something.

**Paul (on Use this pattern):** Phase 3 shows "what changed in one line." Add why. "Version 2: audience is operations leaders, not the board - because the board only approves; operations has to use it." The why is what I'd want to read.

**Gary (adversarial), on Use this pattern:** Phase 4 waits for a round with no change. Put the time box in the prompt: ask at the start how many rounds or how much time the user has, and say so when it's used up.

**Round 2 proposed changes:**
5. Step 5: time-box it - set the number of rounds or a date up front; if it isn't locked by then, the owner decides. (Gary)
6. Step 1: name who has to agree with the destination (the sponsor or business owner), not just the person doing the work. (Anita)
7. Step 2: ask for options that differ on one thing that matters - audience, format, or approach. (Mei)
8. Use this pattern, Phase 3: each change gets a one-line why, not just a what. (Paul)
9. Use this pattern: ask for the time box in Phase 1 and say when it's used up. (Gary)
