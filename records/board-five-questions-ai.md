# Five questions every board should ask about AI

*Draft 1 - November 12, 2026 (overnight). For Greg's review before anything is built. Audience: nonprofit and small-company board members. Each question comes from a Governance pattern at aisuccesspatterns.com.*

You do not need to understand how AI works to oversee it. You need to ask good questions and know what a good answer sounds like. Here are five.

## 1. "When AI saves our people time, what happens to that time?"
**Why ask it:** Time saved is real, but it is not yet a staffing decision. Organizations that cut people before redesigning the work have had to reverse those decisions in public.
**A good answer sounds like:** "We are redesigning the work first, measuring what the freed time is needed for, and only then deciding on staffing - once, with a range we can defend."
**Pattern behind it:** The Productivity-to-Headcount Trap

## 2. "If the person who set up our AI tools left tomorrow, could someone else run them, fix them, or turn them off?"
**Why ask it:** AI tools often live in one person's head. When that person leaves, the tool keeps running while the world changes around it, and nobody knows what to check.
**A good answer sounds like:** "Every AI tool we rely on has a short written record: what it does, what data it can reach, who owns it, and when we review it."
**Pattern behind it:** Ghost in the Workflow

## 3. "Where does AI touch the people we serve - and who decided that was the right place?"
**Why ask it:** The easiest work to automate is often the most visible - the call, the chat, the moment someone needs a person. The work that should go first is the hidden friction behind it.
**A good answer sounds like:** "We are using AI on the handoffs, status chasing and re-keying first. The moments that build trust - a complaint, a hard conversation - stay with people."
**Pattern behind it:** Automate the Invisible

## 4. "Are we measuring what AI changed, or just how much people use it?"
**Why ask it:** Usage numbers go up easily. They do not tell you whether AI is helping or just making small tasks slower and documents longer.
**A good answer sounds like:** "We track impact on a few real tasks - time, quality, value realized - and we have named work where AI should not be used by default, like performance reviews."
**Pattern behind it:** AI Friction

## 5. "When we make decisions, are we hearing our people's own thinking - or the same AI answer in different words?"
**Why ask it:** AI makes each person's work more polished and everyone's work more alike. A board that hears ten versions of one idea has lost the range it recruited for.
**A good answer sounds like:** "People write down their own view before using AI, and we ask AI for a range of options, not the one best answer."
**Pattern behind it:** Ten People, One Idea
