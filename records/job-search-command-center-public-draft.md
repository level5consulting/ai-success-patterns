## Results page story (for the Results page, once it's live)

**One working session, five patterns: a command center for a job search**

The problem: a job search spreads across job boards, applications, emails and follow-up dates. Most of it lives in someone's head or in a chat that forgets.

What Greg built, in one ChatGPT session:
- First, onboarding (Onboard, Don't Program): a role, ranked priorities, criteria, source material, and decision limits - research and prepare freely, approve before anything is sent or changed. About 10 minutes from onboarding to a working setup.
- A private folder of five plain documents - briefing, application tracker, follow-up queue, daily dashboard, and a handoff note covering permissions, dependencies and what to do when something fails.
- A scheduled weekday morning report that reviews the records, calculates follow-ups from verified dates, finds a handful of relevant openings, and presents decisions for approval.
- A real application tracked end to end, including reading the confirmation email and telling an automated acknowledgment from a recruiter's reply.
- An email workflow with a person in the loop: the AI writes the search, Greg runs it in his own mail, and pastes back what matters - simpler and more reliable than a new integration.

| Pattern | How it showed up |
|---|---|
| Onboard, Don't Program | The assistant got a role, priorities, sources and limits before any work. |
| The Chat Is Not Your Memory | The documents hold the records, decisions and handoff - not the chat. |
| The Night Shift | A scheduled morning run prepares research, follow-ups and decisions for review. |
| Ghost in the Workflow | Permissions, dependencies, failure handling and approval limits are written down. |
| The AI Operations Manager | One routine pulls openings, applications, follow-ups and decisions together. |
| AI Friction | An email integration was skipped on purpose; copy and paste was faster. |

Proven so far: the onboarding, the documents, the tracker, the schedule, interpreting real email.
Still to prove: the first unattended morning run, the quality of what it finds, the full follow-up loop.

I onboarded an AI assistant to run my job search. Not a prompt - onboarding: a role, priorities in order, the sources to work from, and one hard line: prepare anything, but nothing gets sent or changed without my approval. About 10 minutes later it had a filing system of five plain documents and a 7 AM weekday report on a schedule. ### If they ask "so what?"

- The human keeps the decisions; the AI does the prep.
- Not everything should be automated - knowing where to stop is part of the pattern.
