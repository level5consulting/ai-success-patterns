## Round 1 - Problem and Value

**Diane (adversarial):** My CEO wants a number by role by June. You're telling me to go map a value stream for three months first. I'll be fired before your map is done. The page needs to say what I can show in the first 30 days - or nobody with my job will try it.

**Ray:** Your RFP story is the whole pattern in four sentences. Put it in the Problem, not the Notes. Every operations leader has lived it: one team gets faster and the queue just moves.

**Priti:** This is value stream mapping - Lean has done it for decades. Say so. People who already know VSM will trust the page more, and people who don't will learn the name. It also fits Greg's tagline.

**James:** The Problem talks about roles like they're boxes on a chart. I'm the role. If you write "redesign the roles last," I read "we'll decide what happens to you after the machine is set up." Say what people in the stream get to do during the redesign, not after.

**Round 1 proposed changes:**
1. Add a "first 30 days" line to Value: one stream mapped, one constraint named, one end-to-end baseline - something a leader can report quickly. (Diane)
2. Move the RFP example from Notes into the Problem as the opening illustration. (Ray)
3. Name it plainly: this is value stream mapping, a long-standing Lean practice, applied to AI. (Priti)
4. In Value or Context, say the people who do the work help map the stream - they're part of the redesign, not its subject. (James)

## Round 2 - Context, Solution, and Use this pattern

**Diane (adversarial):** Step 1 asks for wait times for every step. Nobody has that data. If the page assumes it, people stop at Step 1. Tell them a rough estimate from the people doing the work is enough to find the constraint.

**Priti:** Step 2, "find the constraint," needs one practical test: where does work pile up, or where do people wait? That's how you spot it on the floor without a data project.

**Ray:** Step 3 says "then find the next constraint and do it again." Good - but say how you know you're done with this round: when the end-to-end number moved. Otherwise teams keep optimizing forever.

**James:** Step 5 - roles last - should promise something: the people in the stream see the new flow before their role is redesigned, and the skills gap gets training, not just a new org chart.

**Diane (adversarial), on Use this pattern:** Phase 2 asks the user a lot of questions per step. Let the AI accept "I don't know - roughly a day" and keep going. Mark it as an estimate, but don't stall.

**Round 2 proposed changes:**
5. Step 1: a rough estimate from the people who do the work is enough - you don't need a data project to start. (Diane)
6. Step 2: add the floor test - look for where work piles up or people wait. (Priti)
7. Step 3: a round is done when the end-to-end measure moves - then pick the next constraint. (Ray)
8. Step 5: people in the stream see the new flow before their role changes, and skill gaps get training. (James)
9. Use this pattern, Phase 2: accept rough answers ("about a day"), mark them [Estimate - confirm], and keep going. (Diane)
