Avoid AI Slop with an LLM Interview; interviewer Frank Kowalski (VP Finance, 500-person distributor; four teams claimed AI savings worth more than payroll). Renamed from "Prove the Delta" by Greg: "your are right it is something else. Time Saved is not a measure."
Greg on the result: "this is great and is a shining example of avoiding AI slop. If we had gone with prove the delta it would have been a mish mash of nonsense that people would not understand."

## Greg's answers (word for word, typos fixed only)
1. "Funny you should say this, I just read a post on LinkedIn where an employee of Anthropic claimed that AI saved him 60 hours a week. That is not possible. While AI is saving people lots of time what is difficult for people to quantify and understand is what they are doing with that time. In my case I have been able to write a book and build out AI Success Patterns in months rather than years. That is not a time savings that is a go to market advantage. Most people can't think in those terms. That is the kind of new thinking that people have to use. We are getting something to market, to customer, to someone faster. That is worth money and finance people love to build cash flow models that support that."
2. "Right now I can create 5 patterns a week with AI. If I were writing these in Word documents and then creating web pages with them (the old way) I estimate I could create 1 a week. 20 patterns is 20 weeks the old way vs 4 weeks with my AI helper."
3. "I already met with Jen 3 weeks ago. That meeting would not have happened yet and I have that captured on my results pages. I met with [a partner prospect - Greg to say if it can be named] today, again a meeting that would not have happened. Those meetings in my sales funnel are worth money. A deal with them could be worth [amount - Greg to say if it can be public]."
4. "They use cost savings in a traditional sense. Cost savings in the new AI world is like catching smoke with your hands at best. Time to market, revenue generation and customer satisfaction are going to have to be the bars that finance sets moving forward. Imagine the customer sat values that you can get if you are reducing the time it takes to get your customers answers faster. Imagine the upsell opportunities you will have if you can explain how your products fit their needs better and you have access to information instantly about other customer buying habits that are like that customer before they walk in the door."
5. "Those are not proven benefits, think of the pattern as teaching people a way to fish for those benefits and not actually giving them a fish."
6. (Frank's four teams) "1. If they are saving 10 hours a week drafting replies and assuming their calls take 30 minutes, then they should be taking 20 more calls a week. Their metric needs to be how is AI impacting calls per week. 2. Their metric is quotes per week. If they have not doubled the number of quotes then we need to be using redesign the value stream so upstream they are getting more quoting opportunities. 3. If this is true then we need to make sure we are also looking upstream of them to make sure we are increasing production or increasing our supplier count so they have more work. Again a look at the entire value stream is important for this. 4. Do we need 4 times the content. Is that driving better customer satisfaction or is it making it hard to find things on our site. We need to look again at the entire value stream. If this is true then it is a good thing we have inside sales ready to handle the extra quotes."
7. "There is an Agile principle that work will always fill the vacuum. That work is usually work that the person finds enriching or enjoyable. If they are saving time and we don't have a productive backlog for them then it is on us to make sure that the saved time has valuable work behind it. Whenever someone claims 10 hours of saving we need to look at their role and find 10 hours of organization value we can get from that role."
8. (Opinion) "Time saved is never time earned when left unclaimed. If someone is saving time and has nothing valuable in the backlog they will find ways to use it that might not be valuable and will cause organization friction elsewhere. Imagine the amount of emails a person can generate with 10 hours saved working on something that nobody needs. Those emails cause other emails and suddenly time saved becomes a boat anchor that is too big for your boat with a rope that is too short for the depth of water you are in."
9. (Name) "Time Saved is not a measure."

## Draft 1 (shown to Greg in chat; he said "this is great"). [Synthesized] lines not yet confirmed.
- **Lead** [Synthesized]: Time saved is never time earned when left unclaimed. Measure what the saved time produced - more output, faster to market, happier customers - and make sure there's valuable work waiting to fill it.
- **What goes wrong:** answers 1, 4, 7, 8 (smoke, the 60-hour claim, work fills the vacuum, the boat anchor).
- **What to do:** 1. an output measure for every team, not hours (calls per week, quotes per week); 2. if output didn't move, look at the whole value stream (Redesign the Stream, Not the Role) - the four teams from answer 6; 3. when someone claims 10 hours, find 10 hours of organizational value for that role - if the backlog is empty, that's on us; 4. the bars finance sets from now on: time to market, revenue, customer satisfaction - benefits to fish for, not a fish.
- **An example - Greg's own:** answers 2 and 3 (5 a week vs 1; 20 weeks vs 4; Jen's results; the partner meeting; "that is a go-to-market advantage").
- **When to use it** [Synthesized]: any time someone reports AI time savings, or asks for budget based on them.
