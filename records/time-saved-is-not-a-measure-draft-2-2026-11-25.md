**Time Saved Is Not a Measure**

*Time saved is never time earned when left unclaimed. Measure what the saved time produced - more output, faster to market, happier customers - and make sure there's valuable work waiting to fill it.*

Left unclaimed, saved time becomes a boat anchor that's too big for your boat, on a rope too short for the water you're in. [c18]

**What goes wrong**
People use cost savings in the traditional sense. In the new AI world, cost savings is like catching smoke with your hands, at best. You'll see posts claiming AI saved someone 60 hours a week. The problem isn't whether that's possible - it's that hours saved measures what went in, not what came out. [c4, c14] AI is saving people lots of time. What's hard to quantify and understand is what they're doing with that time.

There's an Agile principle that work always fills the vacuum - usually with work the person finds enriching or enjoyable. If they're saving time and there's no productive backlog behind them, they'll find ways to use it that might not be valuable and will cause friction elsewhere. Imagine the emails a person can generate with 10 saved hours, working on something nobody needs. Those emails cause other emails.

**One exception: overtime saved is a real measure.** If saved time means you stop paying overtime - two people no longer working Saturdays - that's a number on your P&L, in any business. Call it out specifically as overtime saved. Hours "saved" during the normal workweek are still smoke. [c1, Greg's note]

And time is still a valid measure when it's time to market. It's time saved that isn't. [c3, Greg's note]

**What to do**
1. **Pick the first output measure on Monday.** Build real metrics, and real work to fill the time saved. [c5, Greg's note] For each team, choose the output that matters - calls handled, quotes sent, invoices processed.
2. **Get a "before" number.** You need a baseline. If you don't have one, start counting now. [c6]
3. **Compare fairly.** Check output against the same period last year, or against a team not using AI yet, so a busy season or a price change doesn't get credited to AI. [c10]
4. **Pair every output measure with a guard measure:** calls per week with customer satisfaction; quotes per week with win rate. [c12]
5. **If someone claims 10 hours saved, ask "where did the 10 hours go?"** Saved time rarely turns into output one-for-one. [c11] Customer service saving 10 hours on replies might take more calls - or might not, because calls depend on demand they don't control. So start upstream: look at the whole value stream with Redesign the Stream, Not the Role. [c2] If quotes haven't grown, find more quoting opportunities upstream. If accounts payable saved a person's worth of time, look for more production or more suppliers. If marketing makes four times the content, ask whether you need it - is it improving customer satisfaction, or making the site harder to search?
6. **The manager finds the value.** When someone claims 10 hours saved, the manager finds 10 hours of organizational value for that role. [c7] Keep a ready list of valuable work, so the 10 hours aren't invented on the spot. [c9]
7. **Ask about time saved in a way that keeps people honest.** Reward the output, don't punish the honesty, so people keep reporting. [c17]
8. **Set the bars finance should use from now on:** time to market, revenue and customer satisfaction - faster answers for customers, better upsell from knowing how your products fit their needs and what similar customers bought. These are benefits to fish for, not proven ones. The pattern teaches people how to fish; it doesn't hand them a fish.

**Who owns the work / What you stop doing** [c8]
- **Who owns it:** the manager owns the output measure and the backlog of valuable work.
- **What you stop doing:** the "how many hours did AI save you?" survey.

**An example - my own**
I used to publish about one pattern a week. With AI I publish five, so twenty patterns shipped in four weeks instead of twenty. [c3] That isn't a time saving; it's a go-to-market advantage.

How do I know it mattered? I can point to meetings by date that wouldn't have happened yet: one with Jen three weeks ago, captured on my Results page, and one with a partner prospect this week. [c13] That's the test for any revenue or time-to-market claim - tie it to something that wouldn't have happened otherwise. [c15]

A first meeting isn't worth the whole deal, though. Finance discounts a meeting by how often meetings become deals, so don't put a deal value on a first meeting. [c16] Getting something to market, to a customer, to someone faster is still worth money, and finance people love to build cash flow models that support it. Most people can't think in those terms yet. That's the new thinking we have to use.

**When to use it**
Any time someone reports AI time savings, or asks for budget based on them.
