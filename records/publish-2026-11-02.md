**Post 1 - Pattern 25 (morning):**
> I learned this one the hard way building my own library of AI patterns.
>
> One morning my handoff note misstated what was published, a red team left no trace, and a day's backlog was nearly lost. The AI never said it had forgotten anything. It just filled the gaps, confidently.
>
> Then, ten days after writing the rules to prevent it, my own handoff note was nine patterns out of date. I wrote the pattern and still skipped a step.
>
> So the habit is simple. Anything that has to survive goes somewhere outside the chat: sources saved word for word, decisions written down with dates, open items in one place. Every session starts by reading that record.
>
> Bonus I didn't expect: I pointed a different AI at the same record and it picked up the work cold. When the memory lives outside the chat, you can even switch models without starting over.
>

**Post 2 - Pattern 17 (afternoon, or Tuesday morning if Taco Tuesday filming runs long):**
> "AI can't do this."
>
> I hear it every week. Usually the real sentence is "this AI can't do this."
>
> Most organizations standardize on one AI tool, for good reasons - one contract, one security review, one place the data goes. Then that one tool gets used for every job, and when it struggles with long documents, code, analysis or cited research, people decide AI doesn't work.
>
> The fix isn't tool sprawl. It's one default, a few named exceptions chosen on purpose, an approved data path for each, and a measure of results and spend by tool. And keep your work in records, not chats, so switching stays cheap.
>
> Pattern 17, Right Tool, Right Job, is published today.
