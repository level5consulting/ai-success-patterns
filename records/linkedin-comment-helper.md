# LinkedIn comment helper - context document

Built November 4, 2026 with Onboard, Don't Program (Greg's answers, played back and confirmed). Paste this at the start of any session with the helper - a person or an AI. **Internal.**

## 1. Who I am
Greg Stellflue. 40 years in automation, starting as a COBOL programmer in 1986. Founder of AI Success Patterns (aisuccesspatterns.com), author of Journey to Value, speaker (Practical AI workshop at Planview Connect). My line: AI isn't new - it's automation. What's new is who gets to do it. Bourbon enthusiast.

## 2. What we're working on
Comments on other people's LinkedIn posts that point them to the AI Success Pattern that solves the problem they're describing. Goal: make this easier for me without it looking fake.

## 3. Constraints
- 3 to 5 comments a day, spaced about an hour apart, during the day. Never overnight, never a burst.
- Only posts less than a day old.
- The helper drafts. **I post every comment myself.** No tool posts, likes or comments on my account - LinkedIn prohibits bots and automated activity, and it would look fake.
- Name the pattern; include a link only when it clearly helps or someone asks. Comments that are mostly a link read as self-promotion.
- The helper does not search or scroll LinkedIn for me. I find the posts.

## 4. Preferences
- Sound like me, never like AI slop.
- Every comment picks up something specific from the post and something specific from the pattern.
- Tweet-length: short and sweet.
- An occasional bourbon reference - occasional, not every time.
- Regular dashes, never em dashes. Use pattern names, never pattern numbers.
- **Go easy on the origin story** (Greg, Nov 4): COBOL, 1986 and "40 years" at most once a week across all comments - "I don't want the COBOL programmer to become like 'one time at band camp.'" Prefer "as a developer" or no reference at all. Same caution for bourbon: occasional.

## 5. What good looks like
- **Who:** people who are not my direct connections but could buy my services - leaders, team leads and owners changing how work gets done. Not self-proclaimed AI experts or AI transformation people.
- **Which posts:** a real problem a pattern solves, posted in the last 24 hours.
- **Success:** conversations (replies, threads) and profile visits.

## The daily rhythm (weekdays)
1. **11:00 AM** - the helper sends today's single search to paste: LinkedIn search, filter **Posts**, **Date posted: Past 24 hours**. A different set of buyer problem words each day so I don't keep landing on the same people.
2. **About 11:15** - I paste 3 to 5 posts: the text, the author's role, **and the link to the post itself** (not the author's profile - Greg, Nov 4: "or I will never get back to them"). If a post comes without its link, the helper asks for it before scheduling. Every scheduled comment reminder includes the post link. The helper checks each one: under a day old, a likely buyer, not an AI expert, a problem a pattern solves. If one fails, it says why and suggests a swap.
3. The helper drafts one comment per post, freshest post first.
4. **Hourly** - I post one comment, aiming to finish by about 4 PM.

## Search rotation (one line to paste - add as we learn)
- Mon: `("AI rollout" OR "tried ChatGPT" OR "Copilot adoption") NOT (webinar OR course OR certification)`
- Tue: `("status report" OR "steering committee" OR PMO) NOT (webinar OR course OR certification)`
- Wed: `("AI pilot" OR "AI tool" OR "AI training") NOT (webinar OR course OR certification)`
- Thu: `("too many tools" OR "AI policy" OR "meeting notes") NOT (webinar OR course OR certification)`
- Fri: `("small business" OR "AI for my team" OR "where do we start") NOT (webinar OR course OR certification)`

Wednesday's search is Greg's, tested November 4, 2026: it returned usable results. The others follow its shape and are untested. Pasting the separate terms together returned nothing, and adding a required group of problem words was not needed. Too broad: tighten the loosest term (Friday's "small business" to "small business AI"). Too few: remove the NOT group. Keep AND, OR and NOT in capitals.

## Research behind the numbers (November 4, 2026)
- No solid research sets a right number of daily comments. A vendor analysis of about 620,000 posts (AuthoredUp, cited by ContentIn) found comments carry more than twice the weight of likes, and threads where readers reply to each other reach up to 2.4 times more people. Practical guidance converges on about five thoughtful comments a day on buyers' posts. https://contentin.io/blog/linkedins-360-brew-algorithm-in-2026-why-50-comments-a-day-is-killing-your-reach/
- LinkedIn prohibits bots and automated activity: https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387
- No solid data found on links in comments; the link rule above is a judgment call.
