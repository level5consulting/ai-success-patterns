## 2. Talking points (Greg's voice)

**Cold open (0:00)**
- Welcome to A Ten-Minute Taco 'bout AI. Every Tuesday, one pattern, about ten minutes. Short enough for your 10 minute file - the list of things that, if you get ten minutes, help you sharpen the saw.
- [GREG: taco opener - something on Tuesday, or on serving it in ten minutes. Bourbon-post energy.]
- Today: how to stop AI from writing slop in your name.

**The problem (0:45)**
- Hand AI a content task and it writes from its training data - patterns and phrasings it's seen thousands of times.
- What comes back is correct, organized, and completely indistinct. That's AI slop: content anyone could have written, for anyone, about anything near your topic.
- It flattens your expertise and tells every reader who matters that nobody with real knowledge was involved.
- The instinct is to edit it into shape. That costs more than it looks - you fight the AI's structure, and the finished piece still rests on the AI's thinking, not yours.
- Where this came from: watching it at conferences. Polished, AI-drafted decks - and presenters who couldn't explain or defend their own advice in Q&A.
- [GREG: your conference story - the moment you saw it happen. Keep the people anonymous.]

**The value (3:00)**
- Content that reads like you because it is you. Your expertise comes through because it was never replaced.
- Slop has a cost: in a 2025 study, 40% of US desk workers had received AI "workslop" in the past month - about $186 per employee per month in time spent dealing with it - and about half saw the sender as less capable (BetterUp Labs and Stanford Social Media Lab, Harvard Business Review, September 2025).
- And a free credibility test: if you can't answer the interview's question, you shouldn't be making that claim on stage.
- [GREG: a line about being the person who used AI well, not the person AI used.]

**Use this pattern (4:30)** - demo, screen share
- The whole move: don't ask AI to write. Ask it to interview you first.
- Step 1 - paste the interview prompt: "I'm preparing [content type] on [topic]. Before writing anything, interview me. Ask me 10 questions..." Answer two or three live, in your own words. [GREG: pick a real, low-stakes topic you can answer on camera.]
- Step 2 - the draft prompt: "Now draft [the content] using only what I told you in my answers... Flag any section where you're synthesizing beyond what I said."
- Point at the flags: that's the AI telling you where it would have made things up.
- If the interview comes up empty, you're not ready to write yet. Thin answers mean the thinking isn't done.
- Then show the site: Pattern 05 has a Use this pattern box - paste it into any AI tool, or use it as a skill if your tool supports them (Claude and others).
- [GREG: the "it asked me a question I couldn't answer" moment, if it happens live - keep it in.]

**Where this goes next (8:30)**
- The interview does something else: it surfaces the work you actually know needs doing. That's your backlog.
- Next Tuesday: The Night Shift - hand that backlog to the AI overnight and review it in the morning. It took my work on a problem from all day to a couple of hours in the morning.
