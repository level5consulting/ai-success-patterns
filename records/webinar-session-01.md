## Talking points

**Pre-roll (5-10 seconds, not counted)**
- "Welcome to A Ten-Minute Taco 'bout AI. One pattern, about ten minutes, for your 10 minute file." [GREG: taco opener.]

**Act I - The Leader's Anxiety (0:00-1:00)**
- Meet Carla Brennan, CEO. The conference chair calls: "We'd love you to open the event. Your AI journey. Forty-five minutes. Q&A at the end. Next Thursday."
- Her peers will be in those seats. The ones who'll ask the hard questions.
- She has one week and no time. [GREG: a line about what a CEO's calendar looks like - or what Carla says to herself when she hangs up.]

**Act II - The Operator's Grind (1:00-2:30)**
- So Carla does what everyone does. She opens her AI tool: "Write me a keynote on our AI journey."
- Ninety seconds later: twenty beautiful slides. Correct. Organized. Completely indistinct. It could be any company's keynote. That's AI slop.
- She spends the next three evenings editing it into shape - fighting the AI's structure instead of building her own. And it still rests on the AI's thinking, not hers.
- Then Q&A. Someone asks about slide 12. She can't explain it, because she never thought it. [GREG: your real conference story here - the presenter who couldn't defend their own deck. Keep people anonymous.]
- [GREG: humor beat - "the AI was very confident. Carla, less so."]

**Act III - The Hero Moment (2:30-7:30) - live, on screen**
- "Let's rewind the week. Here's what Carla should have done." Slide 3: "Don't ask AI to write. Ask it to interview you first." Then switch to the screen.
- Open a fresh AI session. Paste the "Use this pattern" text from the Pattern 05 page.
- Phase 1 - the AI confirms context. Type: "A keynote for an industry conference on our company's AI journey. The audience is other manufacturing CEOs." 
- Phase 2 - the AI invents an interviewer persona. Read it aloud. [GREG: react to whoever it invents - that's a natural laugh.]
- Phase 3 - the interview. Answer three or four questions as Carla, from a prepared set of answers (see "Prep" below). Show at least one moment where the AI pushes for a specific example - that's the pattern working.
- Say "let's draft." Show the draft for one section.
- Point at the flags: the AI marks where it would have had to make things up. "That's where Carla would have been standing on slide 12 with nothing to say."
- Line to land: "Every word on that screen came from Carla. The AI did the structure. She did the thinking."
- [GREG: humor beat - "Same AI. Same ninety seconds. Completely different keynote."]

**Act IV - The Value Hypothesis (7:30-9:00)**
- Slide 4. Not a promise - a test for your next piece of high-stakes content. Measure three things:
  1. Time to a final draft you'd put your name on.
  2. How much you had to edit.
  3. Whether you could answer every question in Q&A.
- And what slop costs if you don't: in a 2025 study, 40% of US desk workers had received AI "workslop" in the past month, about $186 per employee per month in time spent dealing with it (BetterUp Labs and Stanford Social Media Lab, Harvard Business Review, September 2025).
- A built-in check: if you can't answer the interview's question, you shouldn't be making that claim on stage.

**Act V - The Outcome Realized (9:00-10:00)**
- Slide 5. Thursday. Carla takes the stage. Slide 12 comes up - and this time it's her story, her numbers, her opinion. Someone asks the hard question. She answers it, because she's the one who wrote it.
- "The expertise was already yours. AI just has to ask for it."
- Next Tuesday: the interview also fills your backlog - and in Session 2, The Night Shift, that backlog goes to work while you sleep.
