## Talking points

**Pre-roll (about 10 seconds, not counted)** - Greg's line (October 23), lightly tightened; read it as you like:
- "Those who forget history are doomed to repeat it. Those who repeat history back blindly are doomed to sound hollow. And what is AI but a regurgitation of history? If you don't add your own seasoning, it's hollow. Welcome to A Ten-Minute Taco 'bout AI."

**Act I - The Leader's Anxiety (0:00-1:00)**
- On screen: the calendar invite. "Board of Directors - Thursday 9:00 AM. Agenda item 4: Our AI plan - C. Brennan - 30 min + questions."
- Meet Carla Brennan, CEO of a regional manufacturer. The board chair wants her AI plan. Next Thursday.
- Maybe yours isn't a board. It's the client proposal, the quarterly update to your boss, the budget ask. Same problem.
- Her board will ask the hard questions. She has one week and no time. [GREG: a line about what a CEO's calendar looks like - or what Carla says to herself when the invite lands.]

**Act II - The Operator's Grind (1:00-2:30)**
- Lead with the cost: Carla spends three evenings editing a deck she didn't write. Fighting the AI's structure instead of building her own.
- How she got there: "Write me a board presentation on our AI plan." Ninety seconds later, twenty beautiful slides. Correct. Organized. Completely indistinct. It could be any company's deck. That's AI slop.
- And after three evenings, it still rests on the AI's thinking, not hers.
- Thursday. A board member leans in: "On slide 12 - where does that 20 percent come from?" Carla: "I'd have to get back to you." [GREG: your real story here - the presenter who couldn't defend their own deck. Keep people anonymous.]
- [GREG: humor beat - "The AI was very confident. Carla, less so."]

**Act III - The Hero Moment (2:30-7:30) - live, on screen**
- "Let's rewind the week. Here's what Carla should have done." Slide 3: "Don't ask AI to write. Ask it to interview you first." Switch to the screen.
- "The prompt I'm pasting is on the page - the link is below. Don't screenshot it."
- Open a fresh AI session. Paste the "Use this pattern" text from the Pattern 05 page.
- Phase 1 - context. Type: "A board presentation on our company's AI plan. The audience is our board of directors at a regional manufacturer."
- Phase 2 - the AI invents an interviewer persona. Read it aloud. [GREG: react to whoever it invents - that's a natural laugh.]
- Phase 3 - three interview questions, no more. Answer as Carla from the prepared answers. Keep the one where the AI pushes for a specific example - that's the pattern working.
- Say "let's draft." Show the draft for one section.
- Zoom in on one flag - where the AI marks what it would have had to make up - and hold it for three or four seconds. "That's where Carla would have been standing on slide 12 with nothing to say."
- Line to land: "Every word on that screen came from Carla. The AI did the structure. She did the thinking."
- [GREG: humor beat - "Same AI. Same ninety seconds. Completely different deck."]
- Editing: cut every wait while the AI is typing. Record this act twice and keep the better take.

**Act IV - The Value Hypothesis (7:30-9:00)**
- Slide 4. Not a promise - a test. Last time it took you three evenings. Try it once on your next piece and compare:
  1. Time to a final draft you'd put your name on.
  2. How much you had to edit.
  3. Whether you could answer every question.
- What slop costs if you don't: in a 2025 study, 40% of US desk workers had received AI "workslop" in the past month, about $186 per employee per month in time spent dealing with it (BetterUp Labs and Stanford Social Media Lab, Harvard Business Review, September 2025).
- A built-in check: if you can't answer the interview's question, you shouldn't be making that claim in the boardroom.

**Act V - The Outcome Realized (9:00-10:00)**
- Slide 5. Thursday. Slide 12 comes up - and this time it's her story, her numbers, her opinion. The board member asks where the 20 percent comes from. She answers, because she's the one who said it.
- "The expertise was already yours. AI just has to ask for it."
- One ask: "Use the prompt today. It's on the page."
