## Round 1 - Acts I and II (the setup and the grind)

**Rita (adversarial):** Minute one is a phone call about a keynote. I don't give keynotes, and neither do most of your viewers. If I'm not Carla, I'm gone by 0:45. The stakes have to be mine: a board deck, a client proposal, the quarterly update to my boss. Keep Carla if you like her, but make the deliverable something everybody has had to write.

**Dev:** Act II is the part I recognize - the three evenings of editing. Say that louder. That's the cost I actually pay. "Twenty beautiful slides in ninety seconds" is the hook, but "three evenings fighting the AI's structure" is the pain.

**Hannah:** Act I has no picture yet. "Carla's phone call" is a talking head describing a phone call. Give it one visual: the calendar invite on screen, or the conference program with her name on it. And the pre-roll taco line should be three seconds, not ten - the cooking-show promo already does the taco bit.

**Marcus:** Slide 12 is the best moment in the whole thing. Bring it back. Name it in Act II ("she can't explain slide 12"), and close the loop in Act V. You do both - good - but make Act II's version hurt more: a peer asks a specific question and she has to say "I'd have to get back to you."

**Round 1 proposed changes:**
1. Make Carla's deliverable more universal - a board presentation instead of a conference keynote (or keep the keynote but say "or your board deck, or your client proposal" in Act I). (Rita)
2. Lead Act II's pain with the three evenings of editing, not the ninety seconds. (Dev)
3. Give Act I one visual: the conference program or calendar invite with Carla's name on it. (Hannah)
4. Cut the pre-roll taco line to three seconds; the cooking-show promo carries the taco bit. (Hannah)
5. Make the Act II Q&A moment specific: a peer asks about slide 12 and Carla says "I'd have to get back to you." (Marcus)

## Round 2 - Acts III to V (the live demo, the test, the payoff)

**Rita (adversarial):** Five minutes of live demo is where I'd normally leave. A live AI session is unpredictable - the persona it invents might be dull, the questions might be slow. If it drags, you've lost me. Cap the on-screen typing: speed up or cut the waits in editing, and show at most three questions.

**Dev:** Act IV's three measures are good, but give me one number to beat. "Time to a draft you'd sign" - compared to what? Say: "Last time it took you three evenings. Try it once and compare." That's the test I'd actually run.

**Hannah:** The live demo needs a safety net. Record it twice and keep the better take - it's a recording, not live TV. And put the paste-in text on screen long enough to read, or say "the link is on the page" so nobody tries to screenshot it.

**Marcus:** The flags in the draft - where the AI marks what it would have made up - are the proof for someone like me. Zoom in on one flag and hold it. That's the moment I'd believe it.

**Rita (adversarial), on Act V:** "The expertise was already yours. AI just has to ask for it" - good. But the Night Shift tease and two follow-up links in sixty seconds is a lot. Pick one ask. For Session 1, it's "use the prompt today"; the Talk to Greg link can live on the page.

**Round 2 proposed changes:**
6. Cap Act III's live demo at three interview questions, and cut the waiting time in editing. (Rita)
7. Give Act IV a comparison: "last time it took three evenings - try it once and compare." (Dev)
8. Record the live demo twice and keep the better take. (Hannah)
9. Don't make viewers copy the prompt from the screen - say "the link is on the page." (Hannah)
10. Zoom in on one of the draft's flags and hold it on screen. (Marcus)
11. Close Act V on one ask - "use the prompt today" - and leave Talk to Greg / Book a meeting on the session page. (Rita)
