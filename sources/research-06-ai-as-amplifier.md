Goal (red-team change 2, Omar): a fourth example with financial or legal stakes - someone shipped AI output they couldn't defend, and it cost them. Proposed only.

## Lead case - Mata v. Avianca (S.D.N.Y., 2023) - VERIFIED
- **Facts:** Lawyers Steven Schwartz and Peter LoDuca of Levidow, Levidow & Oberman filed a brief citing court opinions that ChatGPT had invented. Judge P. Kevin Castel found bad faith and fined the firm $5,000 on June 22, 2023. They had to send the opinion and hearing transcript to their client and to each of the six judges falsely named as authors of the fake opinions. They "continued to stand by the fake opinions after judicial orders called their existence into question."
- **Source:** Bloomberg Law, June 22, 2023 - https://news.bloomberglaw.com/esg/chatgpt-phony-legal-filing-case-gets-lawyers-a-5-000-fine
- **Why it fits 06:** Not just an error - they couldn't defend the work when challenged, and doubled down. That's the "walk through it with no screen" test failing in the most public way possible.

## It's still happening - VERIFIED
- **U.S. v. McGee** (S.D. Ala., order Oct 10, 2025): court-appointed defense lawyer reprimanded and fined $5,000 for an AI-drafted motion with fabricated citations he didn't check; one cited case had been reversed by the Supreme Court decades earlier. Must file the order in his cases for 12 months.
- **Oneto v. Watson** (N.D. Cal., order Oct 10, 2025): $1,000 fine plus a required course on using AI in legal practice.
- **Source:** Bloomberg Law - https://news.bloomberglaw.com/esg/fake-ai-citations-produce-fines-for-california-alabama-lawyers

## Larger figure - UNVERIFIED (secondary only)
- **ByoPlanet International v. Johansson and Gilstrap** (S.D. Fla., August 2025): nearly $86,000 in sanctions, per VinciWorks blog - https://vinciworks.com/blog/when-ai-hallucinates-and-lawyers-pay-the-86k-legal-wake-up-call/ . Verify against the court order or a major outlet before use.
