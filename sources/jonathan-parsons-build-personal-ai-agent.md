# Jonathan Parsons - LinkedIn post and infographic, "How to Build Your Personal AI Agent"

---

Most people build an AI agent backwards.

They open a model. 
Type a prompt. 
Call it an agent. 
Then wonder why it falls apart on the third task.

No defined job. 
No rules. 
No memory. 
No idea what it should never touch. 
No plan for when something goes wrong. 
Just a chatbot wearing a fancier name.

I spent 16 years in marketing rooms before I properly understood this. 
Watching smart teams bolt AI onto workflows that were already broken. 
Then wonder why it never turned into real leverage. 
Just another tool nobody trusted enough to actually use.

The agents that hold up are built in a completely different order. 
Running marketing at a global beauty brand, we learned this the hard way. 
We tried the shortcut first. 
Big rollout, no real instructions underneath. 
It looked impressive in the deck and fell apart in the first week of use. 
The teams who won weren't the ones with the flashiest model. 
They were the ones who picked one painful task and built properly around it. 
Everything else came after, not before.

Here's the order that actually works, end to end.

1. Pick one job. 
Research, briefs, reports, whatever hurts most right now.
2. Choose the model. 
It's the brain, not the whole agent.
3. Write clear instructions. 
Role, goal, workflow, rules, limits.
4. Give it knowledge. 
Docs, SOPs, the context it actually needs.
5. Give it tools. 
Search, calendar, code, whatever lets it act.
6. Connect your apps.
APIs, webhooks, MCP servers, automations.
7. Add memory. 
Working, conversation, long term, external.
8. Add planning. 
Let it break a big goal into small steps.
9. Add guardrails. 
Validation, permissions, approval on anything risky.
10. Keep humans in the loop for high risk actions. Always.
11. Automate it once it's reliable. 
Not a day before.
12. Test it properly. 
Accuracy, cost, speed, failure rate. 
Then improve.

Skip that order and you get a chatbot with delusions of grandeur. 
Follow it and you get something that quietly replaces real work.

The marketing directors I talk to most weeks skip straight to step 11. 
They automate first, then wonder why nothing sticks past week two. 
It's almost always the missing instructions or the missing guardrails. 
Rarely the model. 
That part usually works fine on its own.
