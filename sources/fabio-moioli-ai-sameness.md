## Underlying study (verified)
Doshi, A. R., and Hauser, O. P. (2024). "Generative AI enhances individual creativity but reduces the collective diversity of novel content." *Science Advances*, 10(28). 300 writers, 600 raters. Writers with the most AI access scored 8.1% higher on novelty; less-creative writers gained most (up to 10.7% novelty); AI did not help the most creative writers; stories written with one AI idea were 10.7% more similar to each other. ## Original post text

Give ten people the same AI tool, and you may get ten better ideas. But you may also get ten ideas that look surprisingly alike.

This is one of the most interesting paradoxes of generative AI.

A study published in Science Advances found that AI-assisted creative writing produced stories judged to be more creative, yet also more similar to one another.

Individually, we may become more creative. Collectively, we risk becoming less original.

And I believe this raises an important question that goes well beyond creative writing.

As AI becomes part of how we learn, work, solve problems and make decisions, we need to be careful not to confuse better answers with better thinking.

The risk is not that AI will replace our creativity. It is that we may gradually become less aware of how much our thinking is being shaped by the same suggestions, assumptions and patterns.

This is why independent judgment, intellectual curiosity and the willingness to challenge even convincing answers will become increasingly valuable.

We should use AI not only to improve our ideas, but also to question them, explore alternatives and discover perspectives we might otherwise overlook.

The greatest opportunity is not to make everyone think better in the same way. It is to help everyone think better in different ways.
