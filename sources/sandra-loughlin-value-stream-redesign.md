## Original post text

There is limited utility in using AI to redesign work inside current roles. It's not nothing, but it doesn't translate into business value in most cases.

Why? Bc business value is the outcome of value streams--workflows that cut across roles and often span divisions and geographies. Speeding up any step in that process, whether the task is done by agents or people, just means that work backs up at the next step.

In a 10 step workflow, using AI to speed up steps 3 and 6 just makes work pile onto whomever owns steps 4 and 7. If step 3 is a genuine bottleneck, great. But it's often not.

That's why role-first work redesign doesn't necessarily make the overall process go faster. (In fact, we usually see things slow down.)

Work redesign that starts at the value stream is slower but ultimately more impactful. It entails mapping the whole process, noting friction points, human glue, tacit knowledge, critical tasks that require judgment, things that people love/hate, and so on.

👉 Then using AI to augment AND automate tasks
👉 Then iterating on this AI-in-the-process redesign a few times until it is "good enough for now"
👉 Than determining what human tasks are left
👉 THEN refactoring human tasks into new roles and identifying the skills required for them**

Actual, meaningful work redesign takes TIME and process transparency, which is why the most advanced versions of this are in software (SDLC) and product development (PDLC). Bc those workflows are decentlyl mapped and measured already (not to mention full of techies), they have a leg up.

I say all the time that most work isn't like software engineering, and that's true. But in the case of work REDESIGN, the approach taken in software and prodect dev is an excellent blueprint for AI enablement across the business.

To manage complexity, I advise clients to do this messy process redesign in 1-2 Tiger Teams for three reasons:
1️⃣ It's faster
2️⃣You can test new operational, incentive, and talent tactics
3️⃣ You avoid pushing change on the broader workforce too quickly

It's tempting to rush work redesign by tackling what you know today: roles. Resist the urge or manage your expectations for what will result. Individual productivity is helpful, but usually not instrumental in unlocking business value.

* For simplicity, I skipped over the very significant data and knowledge aspects of this. Those are actually the biggest barriers to speed, but unlock the most value.

** Separately, you should be capturing data on the skills and capabilities of the people engaged in the workstream, so you can upskill and match them to the new roles as they emerge
