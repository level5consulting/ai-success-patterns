## Original post text (word for word)

What is Blocking AI Efficiencies?

Anthropic just studied its own workforce, and found HUMAN ATTENTION is the AI bottleneck.

Their agents were working 6 hours for every 1 human hour. Reading everything those agents produced would take up half the working week. No one is doing that.

One engineer described facing "an army of Claudes, with every one asking for my attention."

I'm increasingly thinking the pace of work is going to be the pace of human judgement.

The capabilities of AI are extraordinary, but you can't draft as fast as it can draft.

People are producing more. But the agents can now produce more than people can reasonably review, an that gap is causing big BS risk and burnout.

Because AI doesn't know anything about real judgement or discernment, you have to be able to look at that output and know what's good or what is glitter on 💩 .

I often think, I need to read 3 research papers to properly evaluate something the AI wrote in 30 seconds. Is that a job I have time or the inclination to do? NO I just don't have time.

Words of AI output signed off ÷ minutes spent reviewing it
The average adult reads non-fiction at about 238 words per minute

Speed reading research shows comprehension falls off sharply above roughly 500 wpm. So:

Under 250 wpm: actually reading
250 to 500 wpm: skimming. OK for low-stakes stuff, not for decisions
Over 500 wpm: physically can't have read it properly, so know that is rubber-stamping

For dense material like contracts, policy, research or technical docs, halve those numbers.

Careful evaluation of complex text is closer to 100 to 150 wpm, so anything over 300 is a red flag.

A 5,000-word AI report needs about 20 minutes just to read at normal pace, and more like 40 minutes to evaluate it properly.

Signed off in 5 minutes works out to 1,000 wpm know that nobody read that!

So how do we then design work flow??

What I'd recommend:
> Put review time in the calendar. It's real work, but have a limit, because we can end up in time wasting rabbit holes.

>Cap the agents running at all at once to what our brains can actually check, otherwise you are eating tokens like crazy on stuff you'll never read.

> Review the riskiest output first, not the easiest.

> Protect deep thinking time with no agent pings and no meetings

> Read the research papers you care about yourself so your judgement is deep

> For leaders, track how much your people are reviewing, alongside how much gets produced, if there is a big gap, you're probably putting out docs that have AI hallucinations, = big creditability risk!

## Fits (Claude's notes, October 28, 2026 - for Greg to choose)
- **19 Research Partner** - "read the research papers you care about yourself so your judgement is deep"; the review gap is how fabricated sources ship.
- **15 The Night Shift** - the strongest fit: overnight output only helps if the morning review is real. Cap runs to what you can review; review the riskiest first; a short morning summary. Evidence for its "findings, not changes" and review-gate design.
- **04 The AI Operations Manager** - Anthropic's "agent chief of staff to triage agent requests" and the rise of meta-work are Pattern 04 in the wild.
- **06 AI as Amplifier** - signing off at 1,000 words a minute is rubber-stamping: you can't defend what you didn't read.
- **Possible new pattern or measure:** "words signed off per minute of review" as a check on rubber-stamping.
